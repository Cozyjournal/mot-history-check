How Early Can You Get an MOT? (And Keep Your Renewal Date)

Last reviewed: October 2026 • Published by MOT History Check

The short answer: you can get an MOT at any time, but if you want to keep the same renewal date for next year, the earliest you can take the test is one month minus a day before your current MOT runs out. Take it earlier than that and there's no penalty — but your renewal date will move.

Plenty of drivers leave the MOT until the last few days and then struggle to find a garage with a free slot. Testing early gives you breathing space. You just need to know how early is too early.

The one month minus a day rule

An MOT lasts for a year. GOV.UK says you can get an MOT up to a month (minus a day) before it runs out and keep the same renewal date.

That means the days left on your old certificate aren't wasted. If you test inside this window and the car passes, the new certificate runs right up to the same date next year, just as if you'd tested on the last day.

You can check your exact expiry date before you book — our guide on when is my MOT due shows the free ways to find it in under a minute.

A worked example

Say your MOT runs out on 15 May.

The earliest you can test and keep the same renewal date is 16 April — one month minus a day before 15 May.

If you take the car in on 20 April and it passes, your new MOT still expires on 15 May next year. You haven't lost a single day, and you've bought yourself almost a month to sort out any repairs if the car had failed.

That's the whole point of the rule. It rewards people who plan ahead instead of punishing them.

What happens if you test even earlier?

You can take the test as early as you like. Nothing stops you. But if you test before that one-month-minus-a-day window opens, the renewal date is reset.

GOV.UK gives this example: your MOT runs out on 15 May, but you take the car in on 14 April — two days before the window opens — and it passes. Your new expiry date becomes 13 April the following year, one year minus a day from the date the car last passed.

In other words, you lose the time that was left on the old certificate. Test three months early and you effectively give up three months you'd already paid for.

One more exception worth knowing: if your last MOT was in Northern Ireland and your next test is in Great Britain, the rule above doesn't apply. Your renewal date is based on the date of the GB test instead.

Reasons you might test much earlier anyway

Sometimes losing a few weeks doesn't matter. Common reasons drivers happily test well ahead of time:

  • A long trip is coming up. You'd rather know the car is sound before a holiday or a long drive, not discover a problem the week before.
  • You're selling the car. A fresh MOT with close to a full year on it makes a car easier to sell, and testing early gives you time to fix anything that comes up.
  • You suspect a fault. Testing early tells you where you stand while there's still time to shop around for repair quotes.
  • Your garage gets booked up. Some test centres fill their slots weeks ahead in busy months. An early booking avoids the scramble.

If none of those apply, the smart spot is inside the final month — early enough to fix problems, late enough to keep your date.

Keep your old certificate until the new one is recorded

When you test early, hold on to your old MOT certificate until you've seen the new result recorded. It's your proof of the dates, and it shows there was no gap in cover.

You don't have to rely on paper, though. The official record is held online, and you can check the MOT history of your car free on GOV.UK. It shows every pass and fail, the mileage at each test, and the current expiry date. If the new date looks wrong after an early test, that history page is the quickest way to spot it.

What if the car fails an early test?

This is where testing early really pays off. If your car fails but your old MOT is still in date, and no "dangerous" problems were found, you can still drive the car on the old certificate until it expires. That gives you time to get repairs done at a garage you choose, rather than feeling pushed into whatever the test centre quotes you on the day.

The exception is a dangerous defect. If the car fails with a dangerous problem, it must not be driven until it's fixed — even if the old certificate hasn't run out.

Quick answers

Can I book my MOT two months early?

You can book an appointment whenever you like. What matters is the test date. If the test itself happens more than one month minus a day before expiry, your renewal date will reset to one year minus a day from the test date.

Does testing early ever shorten my MOT?

Only if you test before the window opens. Inside the final month minus a day, you keep every day — the new certificate runs to the same date next year.

My car is nearly new. When is its first MOT?

Most cars need their first MOT by the third anniversary of their registration, then every year after that. Our guide on when is my MOT due explains how to work out the date for a newer car.

Can I drive away if my car fails an early MOT?

Yes, as long as your old MOT is still valid and no dangerous problems were found. The car still has to be roadworthy. If a dangerous problem was listed, it can't be driven until it's repaired.

Where can I check my exact expiry date?

On GOV.UK, free. You'll find the link and the steps in our guide to how to check MOT history.

The bottom line

Test inside the last month minus a day and you keep your renewal date, gain time for repairs, and lose nothing. Going earlier is allowed, but it resets the clock. Either way, don't guess your expiry date — check it free on the official GOV.UK service before you book.
