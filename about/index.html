About MOT History Check

Last reviewed: October 2026 • Published by [ADD AUTHOR NAME]

MOT History Check is an independent consumer guide created and maintained by [ADD AUTHOR NAME] to help UK motorists navigate annual vehicle inspections and used car purchases with confidence. Our mission is to explain technical vehicle testing terminology in plain British English without sensationalism, paywalls, or unnecessary complexity.

Every year millions of vehicles undergo testing across Great Britain and Northern Ireland, generating vast quantities of public roadworthiness data. However, interpreting inspection notes—such as the difference between a dangerous steering defect and an advisory on brake pad thickness—can be challenging for everyday motorists. This resource was built to bridge that gap with verified, practical information.

Who runs this site

This website is edited and produced independently by [ADD AUTHOR NAME], an automotive research specialist and consumer advocate based in the United Kingdom. We are passionate about vehicle transparency, driver road safety, and protecting everyday car buyers from unfair dealer practices and clocked vehicles.

Unlike commercial classified car portals, lead-generation aggregators, or fast-fit tyre chains, we do not sell cars, promote replacement parts, or broker auto finance agreements. Our sole focus is delivering accurate, impartial, and actionable advice to motorists.

Why this project exists

For most households, buying and running a car represents one of the largest ongoing financial commitments after housing. When purchasing a used vehicle, buyers often face an asymmetric information disadvantage: sellers frequently know far more about a car's underlying mechanical weaknesses than the prospective buyer.

The official Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency (DVSA) MOT database provides an invaluable window into a vehicle's mechanical history since 2005. When utilized effectively, this public record reveals odometer anomalies, chronic recurring failures, neglected maintenance, and pending component degradation. We established this platform to give motorists the knowledge required to interpret these records accurately and negotiate from an informed position.

Our strict editorial policy

Trust is the cornerstone of our editorial mission. To ensure that every guide published on this site remains reliable, authoritative, and strictly factual, we adhere to four mandatory editorial principles:

  1. Verified statutory sources only: Every factual statement, statutory fee limit, time threshold, and testing standard is drawn directly from legislation (such as the Road Traffic Act 1988), official DVSA inspection manuals, or published GOV.UK guidelines.
  2. Zero invented statistics: We never publish unverified industry estimates, fabricated fraud figures, or unsubstantiated fail-rate claims. Every statistic featured across our guides must trace directly to a verifiable, dated government publication.
  3. Plain British English: Technical automotive terms (such as track rod ends, anti-roll bar drop links, or particulate filter regeneration) are explained clearly so that drivers of all mechanical experience levels can make informed decisions.
  4. Regular scheduled reviews: All articles and guides are subjected to comprehensive editorial reviews to account for updates to DVSA testing criteria, fee structures, and legal precedents.

Corrections and feedback process

Despite our rigorous editorial standards, regulations and testing manuals are subject to change. If you identify a factual error, an outdated government reference link, or an ambiguous explanation on any of our pages, we invite you to inform us immediately.

Our editorial team investigates all submitted corrections within two business days. When a factual modification is made to an article, we update the visible "Last reviewed" date at the top and bottom of the page to maintain full transparency. You can contact our editorial desk via our Contact page.
