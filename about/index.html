About MOT History Check

Empowering UK motorists with transparent, independent vehicle guidance and road safety insights.

MOT History Check is an independent educational platform built to help UK vehicle owners, used-car buyers, and motorists navigate the official vehicle inspection landscape. We do not charge fees, sell fake records, or replace official services; rather, we provide plain-English explanations of test defects, advisories, odometer verification, and roadworthiness compliance.

Our Mission: Consumer Transparency

Buying a second-hand car is one of the largest financial investments British consumers make. Yet every year, thousands of buyers fall victim to clocked odometers, chronic rust hidden under fresh underseal, or dangerous suspension defects.

Our mission is to ensure every motorist knows how to leverage the official DVSA public database to verify vehicle safety records before exchanging money.

Editorial Policy & E-E-A-T Standards

Our guides are authored and reviewed by automotive specialists familiar with the official DVSA MOT Inspection Manual (Class 3, 4, 5, and 7 vehicles) and the Road Traffic Act 1988:

  • Official Source Verification: All statistics, rules, and defect thresholds are cross-referenced directly with the Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency (DVSA) and Department for Transport (DfT) publications.
  • No Fictional Tools: We never simulate lookups or harvest private user data under the guise of an MOT tool. All queries are guided directly to official GOV.UK endpoints.
  • Clear Distinction of Defects: We educate motorists on the strict legal distinctions between Dangerous, Major, Minor, and Advisory categories introduced under the EU Directive 2014/45/EU revisions.
  • Regular Fact-Checking: Content is reviewed quarterly to reflect updates in UK motoring law, electric vehicle (EV) MOT testing adjustments, and emissions regulations.

Official Independence Disclaimer

MOT History Check is an entirely independent consumer advice publication. We are not owned by, affiliated with, endorsed by, or operated on behalf of GOV.UK, the Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency (DVSA), the Driver and Vehicle Licensing Agency (DVLA), or the UK Department for Transport.
