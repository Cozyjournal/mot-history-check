Wrong Mileage on Your MOT Record? How It Happens and How to Fix It

Last reviewed: October 2026 • Published by MOT History Check

You check your car's MOT history and the mileage is wrong — far too high, lower than last year, or just not a number your car has ever shown. It is a common problem, and most of the time it is a simple human error rather than anything sinister. But it is worth fixing, because an odd mileage on the record follows the car, and buyers read it as a warning sign. Here is how these mistakes happen and exactly how to get the record put right.

How wrong mileages end up on the record

A typing error at the test centre. The tester reads the odometer and keys the number into the system by hand. One wrong digit turns 61,240 into 612,240, or swaps two numbers around. It takes a second to happen and most people never spot it on the certificate.

A replaced instrument cluster or odometer. If the speedometer unit has been swapped, the new one may start at zero or show the mileage of the vehicle it came from. The MOT record then jumps in a way that looks strange but has an honest explanation.

Kilometres recorded as miles, or the other way round. Some vehicles display kilometres instead of miles, particularly imports. If the reading is taken at face value, the number on the record can be well over half as big again as the true miles, or oddly low if it is converted twice.

A genuine rollback. It does happen, just less often than the honest mistakes. That is exactly why a strange reading needs clearing up rather than ignoring.

Why a wrong mileage matters

When you sell the car, the buyer will almost certainly run the same free history check you did. A mileage that drops or leaps about looks like clocking — the illegal practice of winding back an odometer to make a car seem less used than it is. Even when you know the truth, you cannot prove it in a driveway conversation, and the doubt usually knocks money off the price or kills the sale. Dealers and valuation services read the same record, so the error can quietly reduce what the car is worth for years. Fixing it while you still have the paperwork is far easier than fixing it later.

How to get the mileage corrected

The route depends on how long ago the test was. The process is set out on GOV.UK, and our guide on how to check MOT history will help you confirm exactly which test entry is wrong before you start.

If the MOT was within the last 28 days

Go back to the MOT centre that did the test and ask them to update the mileage. They will need to see the vehicle again so they can check the odometer themselves, and they will update the record in the MOT database to the mileage at the time they check it. This is the quick and easy route, so check your certificate as soon as you get home from any test.

If the MOT was more than 28 days ago

You report the mistake to DVSA through the online form on GOV.UK. You will need:

  • your driving licence number
  • the MOT test number
  • the date of the MOT
  • the vehicle's number plate
  • the VIN (chassis number)
  • the make, model and colour of the vehicle

Evidence is not optional here. You must show what the mileage should be, with a date within one day of the MOT test. DVSA accepts one or more of the following:

  • an invoice for the MOT
  • an emissions printout
  • a service receipt
  • a vehicle job card from the MOT centre

If your scan or photo shows personal details such as payment information, blank them out before you send it. Once DVSA has corrected the record, you can check the history again and download a corrected MOT certificate. It is also worth re-checking when is my MOT due at the same time, since you will be looking at the record anyway.

What cannot be changed

You cannot edit the record yourself, and neither can a garage outside the 28-day window — only DVSA can correct older entries. DVSA will not change a reading just because you say it is wrong; without evidence dated close to the test, the record stays as it is. And a correction fixes the record, not history: if the mileage was wrong because the odometer itself was replaced or faulty, the readings before and after may still look uneven, and keeping the invoice for that work is the best proof you can offer a future buyer. Finally, a corrected record does not rewrite tests recorded by other centres at other times — each entry stands on its own.

Quick answers

How do I spot a wrong mileage on my record?

Look at the readings in date order in the free GOV.UK history check. You are looking for a number that falls sharply, leaps by tens of thousands, or simply is not a figure your car has ever displayed.

Can the MOT centre fix a mileage from last year?

No. Test centres can only correct a mileage within 28 days of the test, after seeing the vehicle again. Anything older has to go through DVSA with evidence.

What if I have no evidence for DVSA?

Then the correction is unlikely to happen. DVSA needs proof of the correct mileage dated within one day of the test. A service receipt from the same week or the emissions printout from the test itself is usually the strongest option.

Will a wrong mileage affect my car's value?

It can. An unexplained jump or drop looks like clocking to buyers and valuation tools, and that suspicion tends to reduce offers. A corrected record, or paperwork explaining the change, protects the price.

My car reads in kilometres. What should I do?

Tell the test centre before the test starts, so the tester records the figure properly. Then check the certificate before you leave — a km reading entered as miles is one of the most common causes of a strange record.

Spotted a mileage that does not look right? Check the full record first with the free official service on GOV.UK, then follow the DVSA route above while your paperwork is still easy to find.
