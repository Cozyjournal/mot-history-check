Forgot Your MOT Has Expired? Do These 5 Things Now

Last reviewed: October 2026 • Published by MOT History Check

First, the honest bit: there is no grace period. Not a day, not a week. If your MOT has expired, the car shouldn't be driven — or even parked on a public road — until it passes a new test. The good news is that fixing this is quick if you do the right things in the right order.

Here's exactly what to do.

1. Stop driving the car

GOV.UK is clear: you cannot drive or park your vehicle on the road if the MOT has run out, and you can be prosecuted if you're caught.

There are only two exceptions, and both matter later in this list:

  • driving it to a pre-arranged (pre-booked) MOT test
  • driving it to or from somewhere to be repaired

That's it. A trip to work, the shops or the school run is not covered, even if it's short. If the car is on the street, move it onto a driveway or into a garage if you can. If you can't use the car for a while, our guide on can you drive without an MOT explains the rules in full.

2. Check the exact expiry date

Before you panic, make sure the MOT really has expired — and find out by how much. Memory is a poor guide here. Plenty of people mix up their MOT date with their tax or insurance renewal.

The check takes less than a minute and it's free. You only need your registration number. Our guide on when is my MOT due walks you through it, and you can also look at the car's full record — see how to check MOT history. If the date turns out to be next week rather than last week, you've just saved yourself a lot of stress.

3. Book a test for today, or the soonest you can

This is the step that makes you legal again, so do it now, not tomorrow. Phone a local test centre and book the earliest slot they've got — many garages can fit a car in the same day or the next.

The booking matters as much as the test. The exception in step 1 only covers a pre-booked test, so keep the booking confirmation on your phone in case you're stopped on the way. Drive straight there, by a sensible direct route, and nowhere else.

4. Understand the risk you're driving away from

It's worth knowing why this can't wait:

  • The fine. You can be fined up to £1,000 for driving a vehicle without a valid MOT.
  • Your insurance. Most policies expect the car to be legal and roadworthy. If you have a crash with no valid MOT, your insurer may refuse to pay out, or pay less — and that can cost far more than any fine.
  • Being spotted is easy. Police can check a car's MOT status against the national database in seconds. "I didn't realise" isn't a defence.

None of this is meant to frighten you. It's simply why step 3 comes before everything else on your list today.

5. Set a free reminder so it never happens again

Once the car has passed, take two minutes to make sure you're never in this spot again. The official GOV.UK MOT reminder service is free. You sign up once with your registration number and choose text or email, and you'll get a reminder one month before your next MOT is due — cars also get a second reminder two weeks before, if the car still hasn't been tested and passed.

It costs nothing, and it removes the one thing that caused this problem: relying on memory.

What if the car fails the test?

This is where the rules change, so read this bit carefully. Normally, if a car fails an MOT while the old certificate is still in date, you can keep driving on the old one. You don't have that safety net — your old MOT has already expired.

That means after a fail:

  • You can't drive the car away and carry on using it. The only driving allowed is to or from somewhere it's being repaired, or to a booked retest.
  • If the failure includes a dangerous problem, the car must not be driven at all until it's fixed. Driving a vehicle with a dangerous defect can lead to a fine of up to £2,500, a driving ban and 3 penalty points.
  • The simplest path is usually to let the test centre repair it, or book the repair and retest straight away while the car is already there.

Our full guide on can you drive without an MOT covers fail rules and exceptions in more detail.

Quick answers

Is there any grace period after the MOT expires?

No. The car is out of MOT from the expiry date. There's no extra day or week allowed, whatever anyone at the pub tells you.

My test is booked for Friday. Can I drive the car until then?

No. The exception only covers the journey to the pre-booked test itself (and to or from repairs). Every other journey is an offence.

Can I still tax the car while the MOT has expired?

No — you can't renew vehicle tax until the car has a valid MOT again. If your tax is due now, GOV.UK says to register the vehicle as off the road (SORN) until it has passed.

The car's just parked outside my house. Is that a problem?

If it's on a public road, yes — the rule covers parking on the road as well as driving. Keep it on private land, such as a driveway, until it's tested.

How do I check the date right now?

Free, on GOV.UK, with just the registration number — see step 2 above for where to find it.

The bottom line

Don't drive it, check the date, book the test, drive straight there, then set the free reminder. Do those five things and you're legal again — usually within a day. The official check on GOV.UK is free, so that's always the best place to start.
