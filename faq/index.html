Frequently asked questions

Last reviewed: October 2026 • Verified against official GOV.UK guidance

Checking your vehicle's MOT record and understanding test regulations protects you from severe fines and unsafe used car purchases. Below are 15 detailed answers to the most common questions asked by UK motorists.

General rules and online checks

Is checking MOT history free?

Yes. The official GOV.UK MOT history service is completely free and requires only the vehicle registration number. You do not need to register an account, enter payment card details, or provide personal identity information. Motorists should be wary of third-party websites attempting to charge administrative fees for basic MOT record lookups.

How far back does MOT history go?

For standard cars, motorcycles, and light commercial vans, computerized records on the DVSA portal go back to 2005. Test records for heavy goods vehicles (HGVs), trailers, buses, and public service coaches begin in 2018. If a vehicle is registered in Northern Ireland, test results are available from 2017 onwards.

Do I need the V5C logbook to check MOT history?

You do not need the V5C registration document to view pass/fail results, dates, advisory notices, or mileage readings. The 11-digit document reference number from the V5C logbook is only required if you want to identify the specific test station that conducted the inspection or download a duplicate paper certificate.

How early can I get an MOT without losing my renewal date?

You can have your vehicle tested up to one calendar month minus a day before your current certificate expires and preserve your renewal date. For instance, if your certificate expires on 15 May, you can test anytime from 16 April onwards and your new expiry date will remain 15 May the following year.

What is the fine for driving without an MOT?

Driving without a valid MOT certificate can result in a fixed penalty notice or a court fine of up to £1,000. If your vehicle is found to have dangerous defects, you face a separate, more serious offence carrying fines up to £2,500, a driving ban, and 3 penalty points on your licence.

Failures, defects, and retests

What happens if my vehicle fails with a dangerous defect?

If an MOT inspection uncovers a defect categorized as "Dangerous", you must not drive the vehicle on public roads under any circumstances, even if your existing certificate has not yet expired. Driving a vehicle with a known dangerous defect is illegal and invalidates your motor insurance.

What is the difference between an advisory and a minor defect?

Minor defects are formal faults defined in the DVSA testing manual that are not severe enough to fail the vehicle; they are recorded as warnings that require prompt repair. Advisories represent the testing technician's professional judgment regarding components that are beginning to wear but still satisfy legal safety tolerances on the day.

Can I drive to an MOT test centre if my certificate has already expired?

You can only drive an uncertified vehicle on public roads if you are travelling directly to a pre-arranged, booked test appointment at an authorised test station. The vehicle must be in a safe, roadworthy condition with no dangerous defects, and your journey must be direct with no intermediate stops.

How long do I have to get a free MOT retest?

If a vehicle fails its MOT and stays at the testing station for repairs, a retest is free if completed within 10 working days. If you take the vehicle away and bring it back before the end of the next working day, a retest on certain specified items (such as mirrors, wipers, or tyres) is also free. Other retests returned within 10 working days may be subject to a partial fee.

Does the MOT test check internal engine health?

No. The MOT is an annual inspection of safety and environmental emissions standards. It assesses structural integrity, brakes, steering, suspension, tyres, seatbelts, and exhaust emissions. It does not dismantle or evaluate internal engine health, transmission condition, or clutch wear.

Exemptions, costs, and purchasing

When is a brand-new vehicle's first MOT due?

In England, Scotland, and Wales, a brand-new vehicle must take its initial MOT examination three years from the date of its first registration. In Northern Ireland, private passenger vehicles do not undergo testing until they reach four years of age.

What is the maximum official fee for a car MOT?

The maximum statutory fee set by the UK government for a standard passenger car (Class 4, seating up to 8 passengers) is £54.85. Test stations are prohibited from charging more than this figure, although many garages offer competitive discounted rates.

Which vehicles are exempt from annual MOT testing?

Vehicles first registered more than 40 years ago that have not undergone substantial modifications are exempt from annual testing under the historic vehicle classification. However, vehicle owners remain legally required under the Road Traffic Act to ensure the machine remains in a roadworthy state whenever driven.

Can an online MOT check detect vehicle clocking?

Yes. By examining the chronological progression of annual odometer readings on the DVSA portal, motorists can readily detect discrepancies where mileage has dropped between consecutive tests, indicating potential odometer tampering or clerical logging errors.

Can I check if a vehicle has an outstanding safety recall?

Yes. The DVSA testing service displays active manufacturer safety recall alerts on the vehicle summary page for participating vehicle brands. These safety repairs must be rectified free of charge by an authorised franchised main dealer.
