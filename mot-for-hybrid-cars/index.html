Hybrid Car MOT: What Gets Checked, Emissions and Cost

Last reviewed: November 2026 • Published by MOT History Check

If you drive a hybrid, the MOT is mostly the same test your old petrol car had, same class, same fee, same safety checks. The one real difference is the emissions test, and it is the part most guides get wrong: some say hybrids still get a full emissions test, others say they skip emissions entirely. Neither is quite right. This guide follows the DVSA MOT inspection manual itself, so you know exactly what your hybrid will, and will not, be tested on.

Hybrid MOT in short: the same test, the same vehicle class and the same maximum fee (£54.85) as a petrol car, with the first MOT due when the car is 3 years old. The one big difference: under the DVSA MOT inspection manual (section 8.2), hybrids are exempt from the metered emissions test, no exhaust gas test and no smoke meter test. The tester still does a visual smoke check and checks the engine warning light.

Do hybrid cars need an MOT?

Yes. A hybrid car needs its first MOT by the third anniversary of its registration, and then one every year, just like a petrol or diesel car. That covers every kind of hybrid: a mild hybrid that only assists the engine, a full hybrid like a Toyota Prius, and a plug-in hybrid you charge from the mains. The fuel type changes nothing about the timetable. If you are not sure when yours is due, check the date free on the official GOV.UK MOT history service using just the registration number, our guide to when your MOT is due explains the dates and the early-testing rule.

Hybrids take a standard class 4 MOT, the same class as an ordinary car. The maximum fee a test centre can charge is £54.85, there is no VAT on the test fee, and a centre can charge less, many do. A hybrid MOT costs no more than a petrol or diesel one, and you do not need a dealer or a hybrid specialist to do it: any centre authorised for class 4 vehicles can test a hybrid. Our MOT cost guide shows the full official fee table if you want to check what a centre should be charging.

What a hybrid MOT checks

Almost everything in a hybrid MOT is identical to a normal car MOT. The tester works through the same safety items: brakes and how well they perform, tyres and wheels, steering and suspension, lights and indicators, the windscreen and wipers, seatbelts, mirrors and horn, and the structure of the car for corrosion and damage. As with any car, the test does not cover the condition of the engine, clutch or gearbox, and it is not a service.

The hybrid-specific part is small. The tester visually inspects the high-voltage wiring they can see, checking that it is not damaged or insecure, and checks that the traction battery is properly mounted and secure. That is as far as it goes: the MOT does not test the battery's health, its capacity, or how far the car will go on electric power. A hybrid that has lost electric range but is otherwise sound will still pass. The ordinary 12-volt battery that runs the lights and electronics is checked in the same way as on any car.

The emissions question: what the manual actually says

This is where most online guides go wrong, so it is worth being precise. The DVSA MOT inspection manual, section 8.2, lists the vehicles testers do not need to check for exhaust emissions. Hybrids, including mild hybrids, with electric and combustion engines, are on that list. So a petrol hybrid does not get the exhaust gas test, and a diesel hybrid does not get the smoke opacity test done with a meter. That is the rule as written, and it applies to every hybrid, however new or old.

But "no emissions test" does not mean the exhaust is ignored. Two checks still apply to hybrids. First, the visual smoke check: the tester raises the engine speed, lets it settle back to idle, and looks at what comes out of the tailpipe. If the exhaust gives off dense blue smoke or clearly visible black smoke, the car fails, hybrid or not. Second, the engine warning light: on a petrol hybrid first used on or after 1 July 2003, the tester switches the ignition on and checks that the engine malfunction indicator lamp comes on and then goes off. A warning light that stays on, or never lights at all, is a problem.

So if a garage tells you your hybrid "passed emissions" after a meter test, or another site tells you hybrids are exempt from anything to do with exhausts, both are misleading. Your hybrid skips the metered gas test, but the tester still looks at the smoke and still checks the warning light. A tired petrol engine that burns oil will still fail a hybrid MOT on the visual smoke check, the hybrid badge does not save it.

A note on wording, and on future changes. Some garage websites say a hybrid's emissions are "checked when the engine runs". That describes the visual smoke check above, not a metered test: the DVSA MOT inspection manual, section 8.2, is the rule testers must follow, and it lists hybrids as not needing the metered exhaust emissions test. The government has also consulted on future MOT changes for electric and hybrid cars, including possible battery health checks. None of those changes is in force yet (checked November 2026), if the rules change, this page will be updated.

What hybrids actually fail on

Hybrids fail on the same things cars fail on. An analysis of DVSA test data for 2025 found hybrids had the lowest first-MOT failure rate of any fuel type, 9.9% failed their first test, against higher rates for petrol and diesel cars, and when they do fail, it is usually the cheap items: a bulb out, worn tyres, brake pads getting thin, a wiper smearing. The high-voltage system almost never causes a failure, because so little of it is in the test.

That is good news, and it means the usual preparation works. Run through our pre-MOT checklist a week or two before the test, lights, tyre pressures and tread, wipers, washer fluid, horn, and read the most common MOT failure reasons so you know what the tester will be looking at. On a plug-in hybrid, one extra habit helps: keep the petrol engine in regular use. A plug-in that has run on electricity for months can have stale fuel and an engine that has barely run, which is exactly the kind of thing that shows up as smoke on the visual check.

Hybrid vs electric: the MOT difference

It is easy to mix hybrids up with fully electric cars, but their MOTs are not the same. An electric car has no engine at all, so there is no exhaust, no smoke check and no engine warning light to test. A hybrid still has a combustion engine, so the exhaust system is inspected, the visual smoke check applies, and the warning light is checked, only the metered gas test is skipped. The rest of the test is the same for both: brakes, tyres, steering, suspension, lights and structure. If you also want the electric side of the story, our guide to MOTs for electric cars covers what is different there.

Quick answers

Do hybrid cars need an MOT?

Yes. A hybrid car needs its first MOT by the third anniversary of its registration, and then one every year, exactly like a petrol or diesel car. It takes a standard class 4 test and the maximum fee is £54.85.

Do hybrids get an emissions test at the MOT?

No. The DVSA MOT inspection manual says testers do not need to carry out the exhaust gas test on hybrid vehicles, including mild hybrids, and diesel hybrids skip the smoke opacity (meter) test. The tester still does a visual smoke check and checks the engine warning light.

What does a hybrid MOT check that a normal MOT does not?

Very little extra. The tester visually inspects the visible high-voltage wiring and checks that the traction battery is secure, but the battery's health, capacity and range are not tested. Everything else, brakes, tyres, lights, steering, suspension, is the same as any car MOT.

How much does a hybrid MOT cost?

The maximum fee is £54.85, the same as for any class 4 car, and there is no VAT on the test fee. Test centres can charge less, and many do. A hybrid MOT costs no more than a petrol or diesel MOT.

Do I need a specialist garage for a hybrid MOT?

No. Any MOT centre authorised for class 4 vehicles can test a hybrid, you do not need a dealer or a hybrid specialist. The test is a standard class 4 MOT, so book it the same way you would for any car.

To sum up: a hybrid MOT is a class 4 test at a maximum of £54.85, due from three years old and every year after. It checks the same safety items as any car MOT, plus a visual look at the high-voltage wiring and battery mounting. The metered exhaust gas test is skipped for hybrids, but the visual smoke check and the engine warning light still count, so keep the petrol engine healthy and the car will pass like any other.
