When is my MOT due? Expiry dates and renewal rules

Last reviewed: October 2026 • Published by MOT History Check

Your vehicle's MOT is due on the exact calendar date your current test certificate expires, or on the third anniversary of its first registration if it is brand new. In Northern Ireland, brand-new private vehicles do not require their first test until their fourth registration anniversary.

Allowing an MOT certificate to expire without booking an inspection carries severe legal and financial consequences. Fortunately, UK law provides a convenient early renewal window that permits motorists to test up to one month ahead of time without sacrificing a single day of their certificate validity. Below is everything you need to know about renewal schedules, testing early, and checking your status online.

First test timing and standard annual frequency

In England, Scotland, and Wales, every passenger car, light commercial van, and motorcycle must undergo its first MOT examination precisely three years after the date of its first registration with the DVLA. You can verify the date of first registration in Section 4 of your vehicle's V5C registration document (logbook).

In Northern Ireland, vehicles are administered under the Driver & Vehicle Agency (DVA), where the first test for private passenger cars takes place four years after registration. Following this initial test, vehicles across all parts of the United Kingdom must be re-examined every 12 months thereafter.

The one-month-minus-a-day early rule (with worked date example)

Many drivers mistakenly assume that having their car tested early means their next annual renewal date is pushed forward, effectively shortening their certificate cycle. Under DVSA regulations, this is not the case.

You can present your car for an MOT test up to one calendar month minus a day before your current certificate expires, and your new certificate will preserve the original expiry date for the following year.

Worked date example

Suppose your existing MOT certificate expires on 15 May 2027:

  • The earliest date you can take the test while preserving your renewal anniversary is 16 April 2027 (one calendar month minus one day before 15 May).
  • If your car passes its test on 20 April 2027, your new MOT certificate will be valid until 15 May 2028—giving you nearly 13 full months of test validity.
  • Conversely, if you take the test before 16 April 2027 (for example, on 10 April), your new certificate will expire exactly 12 months from the test date (10 April 2028), resetting your renewal anniversary.

What happens if your MOT expires?

Driving on public roads without a valid MOT certificate is illegal. Automatic Number Plate Recognition (ANPR) cameras mounted in police patrol cars and fixed across the strategic road network continuously scan number plates against the DVLA database, instantly flagging uncertified vehicles.

The consequences of driving without an MOT include:

  1. Court fines of up to £1,000: Driving without a valid certificate typically results in a £100 non-endorsable Fixed Penalty Notice, which can rise to £1,000 if contested in magistrates' court.
  2. Severe penalties for dangerous condition: If an uncertified vehicle is stopped and found to have defects deemed dangerous, you can be charged with using a vehicle in a dangerous condition. This carries a separate fine of up to £2,500 and 3 penalty points.
  3. Invalidated insurance policies: Most motor insurance policies include terms mandating that the insured vehicle remains roadworthy and possesses a valid MOT. If you are involved in a collision without a valid test certificate, your insurer may refuse to pay comprehensive damage claims on your vehicle.

The only legal exception: Driving to a pre-booked test

There is strictly one legal exception permitting you to drive a vehicle with an expired MOT on public roads:

You may only drive the vehicle directly to an authorised MOT test centre for a pre-booked appointment, or directly from the test centre to a repair garage to rectify defects identified during an inspection. If stopped by the police, officers will contact the test station to verify that an appointment was booked in your name before you set off.

Crucial warning: You can never use this exception if your vehicle has been issued an MOT failure notice containing a defect classified as Dangerous. Vehicles with dangerous faults cannot be driven on public roads under any circumstances, even to an appointment; they must be transported on a recovery trailer.

How to check your MOT status online

If you cannot locate your paper certificate, you can verify your exact expiry date within seconds using official government digital tools:

  • Use the official GOV.UK MOT status tool: Visit gov.uk/check-mot-status and enter your registration number to view the immediate pass/fail status and certificate expiry date.
  • Check via our portal: Enter your registration plate in our lookup tool on the homepage or How to check page to review past certificates and expiry records.

Signing up for official GOV.UK text and email reminders

The Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency provides a free annual reminder service. By visiting gov.uk/mot-reminder, you can sign up to receive a free SMS text message or email notification one month before your MOT is due, with an additional reminder sent two weeks before expiry if the test has not yet been logged.
