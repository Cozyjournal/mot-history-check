When Is My MOT Due?

Written by MOT History Check editorial team Last updated: October 2024 Sourced from Section 47 Road Traffic Act 1988

Your MOT is due exactly 12 months after your vehicle's last passed test, or 3 years from the date of first registration if the car was bought brand-new. You can check your official expiry date for free on GOV.UK using your registration plate. You are legally permitted to take your car for an MOT up to 1 month (minus a day) before it expires while preserving your renewal anniversary date.

The Core MOT Timing Rules in the UK

1. The 3-Year Rule for Brand-New Cars

In England, Scotland, and Wales, a brand-new passenger car, van, or motorcycle requires its first MOT test on the third anniversary of its initial registration. For instance, a vehicle first registered on 1 September 2021 will need its first MOT by 1 September 2024.

Northern Ireland exception: Private cars registered in Northern Ireland do not require their first MOT test until they reach 4 years of age.

2. The Annual 12-Month Renewal Rule

Once a vehicle passes its first MOT, its test certificate remains valid for exactly 1 calendar year (12 months), expiring at 23:59 on the expiry date printed on your VT20 pass certificate. There is no grace period in UK law. Once midnight passes, your vehicle is officially untaxed and uncertified for road use.

3. The 1-Month Early Renewal Rule (Preserve Your Expiry Date)

Many motorists mistakenly believe testing early will cut their current certificate short. In reality, DVSA rules allow you to renew your MOT up to 1 month (minus a day) before your expiry date while retaining your original renewal date for next year.

Example:

  • Your existing MOT expires on 15 May 2025.
  • The earliest date you can test and preserve this anniversary is 16 April 2025.
  • If you test and pass on 20 April 2025, your new certificate will be valid until 15 May 2026 (giving you 12 months and 25 days of cover).

If you test earlier than 1 month before expiry (e.g. in February), your new certificate will expire 12 months from the date of that test, resetting your annual anniversary.

Penalties for Driving Without a Valid MOT

Under Section 47 of the Road Traffic Act 1988, driving or keeping a vehicle without a valid MOT certificate on a public road is illegal:

Standard Non-Endorsable Fine

Up to £1,000

Automated Number Plate Recognition (ANPR) cameras across the UK automatically detect vehicles without valid MOTs. Police can issue an on-the-spot £100 Fixed Penalty Notice or take the case to court for a fine of up to £1,000.

Dangerous Condition Defect

£2,500 + 3 Points

If your vehicle has previously failed with a "Dangerous" defect and you drive it, you can be fined up to £2,500, receive 3 penalty points on your driving licence, and face a driving disqualification for driving an unroadworthy vehicle.

Does Driving Without an MOT Invalidate Your Car Insurance?

Yes, in most cases. Most UK motor insurance policies contain a clause stipulating that the vehicle must be maintained in a roadworthy state with a valid MOT certificate. If you are involved in a collision while your MOT is expired, the insurer may reduce their payout, reject accidental damage claims for your own vehicle, or pursue you personally for third-party costs.

When Can You Legally Drive Without an MOT?

There is only one legal exemption in UK law:

You may only drive an uncertified vehicle on public roads if you are travelling directly to or from a pre-arranged MOT test appointment at an authorised testing station, or travelling to a garage to have pre-arranged repairs carried out following a test failure. You must be able to prove this booking to police if stopped.
