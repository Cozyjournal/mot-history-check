MOT test costs, fee limits, and retest rules

Last reviewed: November 2026 • Published by MOT History Check

The maximum fee set by the UK government for a standard passenger car MOT is £54.85, and test stations are legally prohibited from charging more than this figure. However, because test centres operate as competitive commercial businesses, many garages charge lower prices or offer discounts when combined with a routine service.

Understanding official statutory limits and how re-examination charges work ensures you never pay more than necessary. Below is the complete breakdown of official maximum MOT fees by vehicle class, why garage charges vary, and the exact GOV.UK rules governing free and discounted retests.

Statutory maximum MOT test fees

MOT fees are capped by the Department for Transport and the Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency (DVSA). Because the MOT test is a statutory inspection mandated by Parliament, no VAT is charged on the test fee itself.

The official maximum fees across standard vehicle classes are as follows:

Vehicle class Vehicle type & description Maximum statutory fee
Class 1 & 2 Motorcycles with engine size up to 200cc (Class 1) and over 200cc (Class 2) £29.65
Class 1 & 2 Motorcycles with a sidecar £37.80
Class 3 3-wheeled vehicles up to 450kg unladen weight £37.80
Class 3 3-wheeled vehicles over 450kg unladen weight £54.85
Class 4 Standard passenger cars, taxis, hybrid cars, and light motor caravans (up to 8 passenger seats) £54.85
Class 4 Light commercial goods vans (up to 3,000kg design gross weight) £54.85
Class 4 Private passenger vehicles and ambulances (9 to 12 passenger seats) £57.30
Class 5 Private passenger minibuses and coaches (up to 16 passenger seats) £59.55
Class 5 Private passenger vehicles and ambulances (more than 16 passenger seats) and playbuses £80.65
Class 7 Commercial goods vehicles (over 3,000kg up to 3,500kg design gross weight) £58.60

The test itself takes 45 to 60 minutes for a standard car — the fee above is for the inspection, not the time. Our guide to how long an MOT takes explains what happens during that hour, how long each vehicle class takes, and how long a retest takes if the car fails.

Official statutory fees verified against current GOV.UK MOT testing fee tables.

These maximum fees have not changed since 6 April 2010, according to the DVSA fee poster — the longest freeze of any major motoring charge. Trade bodies have asked the government to raise the cap (the Independent Garage Association suggested £95), and the DVSA discussed it with the trade at a roundtable in October 2026, but no rise had been agreed as of November 2026. The cap stays at £54.85 for a car until the government says otherwise.

Does an MOT cost the same for diesel, electric and hybrid cars?

Yes. The fee is set by vehicle class, not by fuel type or emissions. A petrol, diesel, hybrid or fully electric car is tested as a Class 4 vehicle, so the maximum fee is £54.85 for all of them. The same applies to vans: a diesel van and an electric van in the same weight class pay the same maximum.

What changes between fuel types is what the tester checks, not what you pay. A diesel car MOT includes a smoke test and a diesel particulate filter (DPF) check, an electric car MOT skips the emissions test entirely, and a hybrid car MOT tests both the petrol engine and the electric side. None of that changes the price — if a garage tries to charge extra "because it's electric" or "because it's a diesel", that is above the legal maximum for a Class 4 test.

What the MOT fee covers — and what it doesn't

The fee pays for the inspection only: roughly 45 to 60 minutes of checks on brakes, tyres, lights, steering, suspension, exhaust emissions, seatbelts and bodywork. It does not cover:

  • Repairs or parts. If the car fails on worn brake pads, a blown bulb or a cracked spring, the parts and labour to fix them are charged separately — and unlike the test fee, repair prices are not capped.
  • VAT on the test. There is no VAT on the MOT test fee itself, but VAT does apply to any repair work.
  • A second full test after a fail. If the car fails, a retest is a separate matter — see the retest rules below. A free or half-price retest is possible in many cases, but only if you return to the same test centre inside the time limits.

That is why the "cheap MOT" and the final bill are two different things. A £30 test that finds £200 of repairs costs more overall than a £54.85 test on a car that passes first time, which is why a quick check of the car before the test is worth doing. MOT costs in Northern Ireland

Northern Ireland runs a different system from England, Scotland and Wales. There are no private MOT stations: every test is carried out by the Driver and Vehicle Agency (DVA) at a government test centre, and the fee is fixed — it does not vary by centre and there is no discount to shop around for. You book through nidirect, and the current fees are:

Vehicle Full test Retest
Motorcycle (Class I and II) £34.00 £29.50
Light motor vehicle (Class III) and motor car (Class IV) £38.00 £29.50
Light goods vehicle (up to 3,500kg) £38.00 £29.50

So a car MOT in Northern Ireland costs £38.00 — less than the £54.85 maximum in Great Britain, but a fixed price, not a ceiling. If the vehicle fails, you must apply for the retest within 21 days or pay the full fee again. Cancel without giving at least one clear day's notice and you lose the fee. Cars in Northern Ireland also have their first test at four years old, not three.

Why garage prices vary across the UK

While £54.85 represents the maximum legal ceiling for standard cars, garages are entirely free to set their prices below this cap. Common factors that influence the price you pay include:

  • Service and MOT bundles: Many independent and franchised service garages discount the MOT test (often charging between £30 and £45) when booked alongside an annual oil service or major scheduled maintenance check.
  • Loss-leader business models: Some high-street fast-fit tyre and exhaust chains offer reduced MOT fees as a way to attract customers through their doors, anticipating they may gain revenue from repair work on worn tyres, brake pads, or wipers.
  • Franchised main dealerships: Main dealer workshops for prestige brands generally charge the full statutory maximum fee of £54.85, reflecting higher workshop hourly labour overheads.
  • Council-run MOT test stations: Many local borough and municipal councils operate their own vehicle maintenance depots to test council fleet vehicles (such as community minibuses and refuse trucks). By law, these council depots are open to the general public for MOT testing. Because council stations do not offer commercial repair services, their inspectors have no financial incentive to discover non-essential faults, making them a popular choice for impartial testing at standard statutory fee rates.

How much is an MOT retest? The official rules and fees

Short answer: an MOT retest is often free. If it is not free, a partial retest within 10 working days costs at most half the original fee — for a car, no more than £27.43 (half of the £54.85 maximum). After 10 working days you need a full test and pay the full fee again. The four official cases:

If your vehicle fails its MOT, you do not automatically have to pay another full test fee to get a pass certificate. The Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency has established precise rules governing re-examinations:

1. Vehicle left at the test centre for repair: FREE retest

If you leave your vehicle at the testing station that conducted the original inspection to carry out the required repair work, the garage can conduct a partial retest completely free of charge, provided the retest is completed within 10 working days of the initial failure.

2. Vehicle taken away and returned before the end of the next working day: FREE retest on specific items

If you take the vehicle away from the test station to fix the faults yourself or have another garage do the work, you are entitled to a free partial retest if you bring the car back before the end of the next working day, provided the failure was solely due to one or more items from the official list:

  • Access doors and fuel filler caps
  • Boot lid, tailgate, and bonnet latches
  • Horn operation
  • Registration plates and lamps
  • Mirrors and windscreen wipers or washers
  • Seats and seatbelts
  • Tyres and wheels
  • Direction indicators and hazard warning lights
  • Brake pedal anti-slip pads

3. Vehicle returned within 10 working days: Partial fee

If you take the vehicle away and return it to the original test centre within 10 working days for defects not covered under the one-day list, the testing station is entitled to charge a partial retest fee. Under official rules, this partial fee is capped at no more than half the original test fee.

4. Vehicle returned after 10 working days: Full test required

If the vehicle is presented for re-inspection after 10 working days have elapsed from the date of the original failure, a complete new MOT examination must be performed and the test station will charge their full standard fee.

How to pay less for your MOT

Because the fee is a maximum, not a set price, and there is no minimum, you can usually pay well under £54.85 for a car:

  • Book the test with a service. Garages commonly discount the MOT to around £30–£45 when it is booked alongside an annual or interim service.
  • Compare a few local prices. Independent garages, fast-fit chains and main dealers all charge different amounts for the same test. Ring two or three and ask for their MOT-only price.
  • Use a council test centre. Council depots test their own fleets and are open to the public by law. They do not do repairs, so they have no reason to find extra work — see the section above.
  • Pass first time. The cheapest MOT is the one you only pay for once. Run through the pre-MOT checklist (lights, tyres, wipers, washer fluid, horn, mirrors) before you book, and book early enough that a fail still leaves time for a free retest — our guide to how to book an MOT test explains the renewal window.
  • Check what a "free" or very cheap MOT includes. Some garages advertise a free MOT with a service. That is a genuine promotion, not a government scheme — there is no free MOT from the DVSA. Read what is included before you book, and remember the retest rules above still apply if the car fails.

Can you appeal an MOT test fee or failure decision?

If your vehicle fails an MOT and you believe the technician graded a component incorrectly (for example, failing a roadworthy suspension bush as a major defect), you have the legal right to appeal before repairs are undertaken. You must submit an official appeal form (form VT17 for cars) to the DVSA within 14 working days of the test. The DVSA will schedule an independent re-examination; if your appeal is upheld, your test fee is refunded.

MOT cost FAQs

How much is an MOT for a car in 2026?

The maximum a garage can legally charge for a car MOT in England, Scotland and Wales is £54.85, with no VAT. Most drivers pay less — typically £30 to £50 — because garages compete below the cap. In Northern Ireland the fixed DVA fee for a car is £38.00.

Do I pay VAT on an MOT?

No. There is no VAT on the MOT test fee itself. VAT does apply to any repair work the garage carries out.

How much is an MOT retest fee if my car fails?

Not always. If the car stays at the test centre for repair, the retest is free within 10 working days. If you take it away and bring it back before the end of the next working day for certain simple faults (lights, tyres, wipers, mirrors and similar), the retest is also free. Otherwise a partial retest within 10 working days costs at most half the original fee, and after 10 working days you pay for a full test again. The full rules are in the retest section above.

Does an electric car MOT cost less than a petrol or diesel one?

No. The fee is set by vehicle class, not fuel type. An electric, hybrid, petrol or diesel car is a Class 4 vehicle, so the maximum fee is £54.85 for all of them. The emissions test is skipped on a fully electric car, but the price is the same.

Can I get a free MOT?

There is no free MOT from the government or the DVSA. Some garages offer a free or heavily discounted MOT as a promotion, usually when booked with a service — that is a commercial offer, so check what it includes before you book.

Has the MOT price gone up?

No. The maximum car MOT fee has been £54.85 since 6 April 2010. Trade bodies have asked for a rise, and the DVSA discussed it with the trade in October 2026, but no increase had been agreed as of November 2026.

Do I have to pay for the MOT if my car fails?

Yes. The fee pays for the inspection, not for a pass — so you pay the test fee even if the car fails. What you may not have to pay again is the retest: if the car stays at the test centre for repair, or you bring it back before the end of the next working day for certain simple faults, the partial retest is free, and otherwise it costs at most half the original fee within 10 working days.

Can a garage charge more than the £54.85 maximum?

No. The £54.85 maximum is set by law, and a test station cannot charge more than that for the test itself — so if a garage quotes above the cap for the test, question it, and you can contact DVSA if you are unhappy with the service. Repair work is separate and is not capped, so a quote above £54.85 that includes repairs is a different matter.
