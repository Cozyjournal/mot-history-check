MOT test costs, fee limits, and retest rules

Last reviewed: October 2026 • Published by MOT History Check

The maximum fee set by the UK government for a standard passenger car MOT is £54.85, and test stations are legally prohibited from charging more than this figure. However, because test centres operate as competitive commercial businesses, many garages charge lower prices or offer discounts when combined with a routine service.

Understanding official statutory limits and how re-examination charges work ensures you never pay more than necessary. Below is the complete breakdown of official maximum MOT fees by vehicle class, why garage charges vary, and the exact GOV.UK rules governing free and discounted retests.

Statutory maximum MOT test fees

MOT fees are capped by the Department for Transport and the Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency (DVSA). Because the MOT test is a statutory inspection mandated by Parliament, no VAT is charged on the test fee itself.

The official maximum fees across standard vehicle classes are as follows:

Vehicle class Vehicle type & description Maximum statutory fee
Class 1 & 2 Motorcycles with engine size up to 200cc (Class 1) and over 200cc (Class 2) £29.65
Class 4 Standard passenger cars, taxis, and light motor caravans (up to 8 passenger seats) £54.85
Class 4 Light commercial goods vans (up to 3,000kg design gross weight) £54.85
Class 5 Private passenger minibuses and coaches (up to 16 passenger seats) £59.55
Class 7 Commercial goods vehicles (over 3,000kg up to 3,500kg design gross weight) £58.60

Why garage prices vary across the UK

While £54.85 represents the maximum legal ceiling for standard cars, garages are entirely free to set their prices below this cap. Common factors that influence the price you pay include:

  • Service and MOT bundles: Many independent and franchised service garages discount the MOT test (often charging between £30 and £45) when booked alongside an annual oil service or major scheduled maintenance check.
  • Loss-leader business models: Some high-street fast-fit tyre and exhaust chains offer reduced MOT fees as a way to attract customers through their doors, anticipating they may gain revenue from repair work on worn tyres, brake pads, or wipers.
  • Franchised main dealerships: Main dealer workshops for prestige brands generally charge the full statutory maximum fee of £54.85, reflecting higher workshop hourly labour overheads.
  • Council-run MOT test stations: Many local borough and municipal councils operate their own vehicle maintenance depots to test council fleet vehicles (such as community minibuses and refuse trucks). By law, these council depots are open to the general public for MOT testing. Because council stations do not offer commercial repair services, their inspectors have no financial incentive to discover non-essential faults, making them a popular choice for impartial testing at standard statutory fee rates.

Official GOV.UK rules for MOT retests

If your vehicle fails its MOT, you do not automatically have to pay another full test fee to get a pass certificate. The Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency has established precise rules governing re-examinations:

1. Vehicle left at the test centre for repair: FREE retest

If you leave your vehicle at the testing station that conducted the original inspection to carry out the required repair work, the garage can conduct a partial retest completely free of charge, provided the retest is completed within 10 working days of the initial failure.

2. Vehicle taken away and returned before the end of the next working day: FREE retest on specific items

If you take the vehicle away from the test station to fix the faults yourself or have another garage do the work, you are entitled to a free partial retest if you bring the car back before the end of the next working day, provided the failure was solely due to one or more items from the official list:

  • Access doors and fuel filler caps
  • Boot lid, tailgate, and bonnet latches
  • Horn operation
  • Registration plates and lamps
  • Mirrors and windscreen wipers or washers
  • Seats and seatbelts
  • Tyres and wheels
  • Direction indicators and hazard warning lights
  • Brake pedal anti-slip pads

3. Vehicle returned within 10 working days: Partial fee

If you take the vehicle away and return it to the original test centre within 10 working days for defects not covered under the one-day list, the testing station is entitled to charge a partial retest fee. Under official rules, this partial fee is capped at no more than half the original test fee.

4. Vehicle returned after 10 working days: Full test required

If the vehicle is presented for re-inspection after 10 working days have elapsed from the date of the original failure, a complete new MOT examination must be performed and the test station will charge their full standard fee.

Can you appeal an MOT test fee or failure decision?

If your vehicle fails an MOT and you believe the technician graded a component incorrectly (for example, failing a roadworthy suspension bush as a major defect), you have the legal right to appeal before repairs are undertaken. You must submit an official appeal form (form VT17 for cars) to the DVSA within 14 working days of the test. The DVSA will schedule an independent re-examination; if your appeal is upheld, your test fee is refunded.
