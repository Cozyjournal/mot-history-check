Can You Sell a Car Without an MOT? The Rules Explained

Last reviewed: November 2026 • Published by MOT History Check

Yes, you can sell a car without an MOT — there is no law against it. But the sale comes with rules: you must tell the buyer the car has no valid MOT, the buyer cannot drive it away on the road, and you will almost always get a lower price than you would with a fresh certificate. This guide explains what the law says, where you can sell, how the car has to be collected, and the DVLA steps once the sale is done.

The key point is that the law controls driving a car without an MOT, not selling one. Driving a car without a valid MOT can mean a fine of up to £1,000. Selling is different — it is a private transaction, and the buyer takes on the car as it is, as long as you have been honest about its condition. Is it legal to sell a car without an MOT?

Yes. There is no law that says a car must have a valid MOT before it can change hands. Cars are sold without MOTs every day — cars that have failed the test, cars that have been off the road for months, and cars being sold for parts or repair. What the law does require is honesty: the buyer must know what they are buying.

That honesty matters twice over. First, a buyer who is misled — who is told the car has a valid MOT when it does not, or who is not told the MOT has expired — has been misled under consumer law, and you could be liable. Second, a buyer can check the car’s MOT status themselves in seconds on GOV.UK, so hiding it does not work anyway. If you are the buyer rather than the seller, our guide to checking a used car’s MOT history shows you exactly what to look for before you hand over any money.

What you must tell the buyer

Be clear about all of this, in the advert and in person:

That the car has no valid MOT. Say so plainly in the advert — “no MOT” in the listing, not buried in small print.

Why it has no MOT. Has it simply expired, or did the car fail? If it failed, say what it failed on. The failure record is on GOV.UK for anyone to see, so there is nothing to gain by hiding it.

What else is wrong with it. Any fault you know about — advisories from the last test, warning lights, mechanical problems — should be disclosed. A buyer who finds a fault you knew about and hid can come back to you.

That the car cannot be driven away. The buyer needs to bring a trailer or arrange recovery. Make that clear before they travel to see the car, or you will both waste a journey.

How the buyer collects the car

A car without a valid MOT cannot be driven on a public road, with only two exceptions: to a pre-arranged MOT test, or to or from a place of repair. Neither exception covers driving a newly bought car home. So the buyer has to collect the car with all four wheels off the ground — on a trailer, a flatbed or a recovery truck.

That also means no test drives on the road. A buyer can start the car and look it over on your driveway, but they cannot take it for a spin around the block. And before they can drive it at all, they will need three things in place: a passed MOT, vehicle tax, and insurance. You cannot tax a vehicle without a valid MOT, so the buyer’s first jobs after collection are the repairs, the MOT test, and then the tax. If the car has been off the road, our guide to SORN car MOT rules explains how the SORN, tax and MOT fit together.

Where can you sell a car without an MOT?

Private sale. Advertising on sites like AutoTrader or eBay Motors, or on Facebook Marketplace, usually gets you the most money — but only if the advert is completely honest about the car’s condition and MOT status. State “no MOT” clearly and list the known faults.

Car dealer or part exchange. Some dealers will take a car without an MOT, either as a trade-in or for stock, but many do not want the hassle and expense of fixing it. If a dealer does offer to buy it, expect a low offer — they are pricing in the repair work.

Car-buying services. Online car-buying services will buy cars without a valid MOT, and they usually collect the car themselves. You will need the V5C logbook and the other documents, and the price will reflect the car’s condition.

Auction. An auction can be a quick way to sell, especially for a car that needs work. The buyer at an auction is usually a trader who knows what they are taking on.

Scrap. If the car is at the end of its life, an Authorised Treatment Facility can collect it and will usually issue a Certificate of Destruction within seven days. You must still tell the DVLA. If the car failed its MOT, our guide to what to do if your car fails its MOT covers the retest and repair options before you decide to scrap it.

What it does to the price

Expect less — sometimes a lot less. A buyer is paying for the car plus the repairs, the MOT test, the hassle of arranging transport, and the risk that the car fails on something expensive. That risk is priced into every offer you get.

So before you sell as-is, do the sums. If the car only needs small fixes — a bulb, a wiper blade, a tyre — a fresh MOT costs at most £54.85 for a car, and the certificate usually adds far more than that to the selling price. Our guide to MOT costs lists the maximum fees for every vehicle class. If the repairs are expensive, selling without an MOT may be the better deal — but price the car honestly, and let the buyer’s own mechanic confirm what is wrong if they want to.

SORN cars and classic cars

SORN cars. If your car is declared SORN — off the road — you can sell it, but it must stay off the road. The buyer has to arrange transport, because the car cannot be driven without an MOT and tax. Be fully honest about the SORN status in the advert, and tell the DVLA about the sale straight away so the SORN ends.

Classic cars over 40 years old. Cars built or first registered more than 40 years ago do not need an MOT at all, so they can be sold — and driven — without one. The buyer still needs tax and insurance. Our guide to MOT exemptions explains the 40-year rule and the other exemptions.

After the sale: tell the DVLA

Whenever you sell a car, with or without an MOT, you must tell the DVLA straight away — online or by filling in the V5C logbook. If you do not, you can still be held responsible for the car, including any fines or tax. Keep a record of the sale: the buyer’s name and address, the date, and the price. And remember that if you have lost the MOT certificate, a replacement costs £10 from any MOT test centre — see our guide to a lost MOT certificate — which can help the sale even if the certificate itself has expired, because it proves the car’s test history.

Quick answers

Can I sell a car that failed its MOT?

Yes. A car that has failed its MOT can still be sold, as long as you tell the buyer it has failed and what it failed on. The buyer cannot drive it away on the road — it must be collected on a trailer or recovery vehicle — and they will need to fix the faults and pass a new MOT before they can tax and drive it.

Can the buyer drive the car home after buying it without an MOT?

No. It is illegal to drive a car on a public road without a valid MOT, except to a pre-arranged MOT test or to or from a place of repair. The buyer must collect the car on a trailer or recovery vehicle, and can only drive it once it has a valid MOT, tax and insurance.

Do I need to tell the DVLA when I sell a car without an MOT?

Yes. Whenever you sell a car, with or without an MOT, you must tell the DVLA straight away — online or by filling in the V5C logbook. If you do not, you can still be held responsible for the car, including any fines or tax.

Can I sell a SORN car?

Yes. A car declared SORN (off the road) can be sold, but it must stay off the road — the buyer has to arrange for it to be towed or transported, because it cannot be driven without an MOT and tax. Be fully honest about its SORN status in the advert.

Will I get less money for a car without an MOT?

Almost always, yes. Buyers pay less for a car with no MOT because they are taking on the repair costs and the risk that it will fail. If the car only needs small fixes, a fresh MOT — which costs at most £54.85 for a car — often pays for itself in a higher selling price.

So, can you sell a car without an MOT? Yes — it is legal, and it happens every day. The rules are simple: tell the buyer everything, do not let anyone drive the car on the road, and tell the DVLA as soon as the sale is done. And if the car only needs a small fix, get the MOT first — a valid certificate is usually worth far more than it costs.
