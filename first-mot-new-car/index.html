When Does a New Car Need Its First MOT?

Last reviewed: October 2026 • Published by MOT History Check

The quick answer: by the third anniversary of the day it was first registered. A car registered on 20 March 2023 needed its first MOT by 20 March 2026. Before that date it does not need a test at all, and there is no record to check. After the first test, the MOT becomes a yearly habit for the rest of the car's life. This page covers the rule, the exceptions, and how to find your car's exact date so the deadline never creeps up on you.

The three-year rule, in plain English

In England, Scotland and Wales, cars, motorcycles and vans are first tested when they reach three years old, counted from the date of first registration — not the date the car was built, and not the date you bought it. If you buy a car that is already two years old, its first MOT is due one year later, on the third anniversary of its registration. The registration date is printed on the V5C log book, and it is also the date that drives everything else in this article. Our page on when is my MOT due explains how the yearly cycle works once the tests have started.

Northern Ireland works to a different timetable: the first test there is due after four years. If your vehicle is registered and tested in Northern Ireland, work to the four-year rule instead.

Vehicles that are tested sooner

Not everything gets three years. Some vehicles must be tested from one year old because of the work they do. On GOV.UK's fees table, the vehicles with a first test at one year include ambulances and taxis, and larger passenger vehicles such as minibuses carrying nine or more seats. Ordinary cars, motor caravans, motorcycles, and goods vans up to 3,500kg follow the standard three-year rule. If you run a vehicle for hire or carry passengers for a living, check the table rather than assuming you have three years. Some vehicles never need an MOT at all — certain historic vehicles are the best-known example — and our guide to MOT exemptions covers who is excused.

How to check your car's exact date on GOV.UK

You do not have to work the date out from paperwork and hope you got it right. The official service will tell you:

  1. Go to the MOT history service on GOV.UK.
  2. Enter the car's number plate.
  3. The result shows the vehicle's details and when its MOT is due — for a car approaching its first test, this gives you the date to work to.

For a brand-new car there may be no test history to show, which is expected and nothing to worry about. Our walkthrough of how to check MOT history covers what each result means once the tests begin.

Set a reminder so you do not forget

The first MOT catches people out precisely because there have been three quiet years with no test, no certificate and no garage prompting them. Two easy safeguards:

  • Use the free GOV.UK reminder service. You can sign up to get a reminder that your MOT is due, by text message or email. It costs nothing and takes a couple of minutes to set up.
  • Put the date in your phone calendar with an alert a month before, at the same time as you do the tax and insurance dates.

That month of warning matters, because it is also your booking window, as the next section explains.

What happens near the deadline

You can have the car tested up to a month (minus a day) before the due date and keep the same renewal date for the following year. So a first MOT due on 20 March can be done from late February onwards without losing any time. That window is the sensible time to book: if the car fails, you still have days in hand to get it repaired instead of a scramble on the last day.

Leave it too late and the position is simple and strict. Once the date passes with no pass, the car has no valid MOT. You cannot legally drive it on the road, and you cannot park it on the road either. The only driving allowed is to a pre-arranged MOT test, or to and from a place of repair. Driving without a valid MOT can bring a fine of up to £1,000, and you also cannot renew the vehicle's tax until it has passed. If the date has already slipped past, book the test first, then drive straight there.

Quick answers

My car is three years old next month. When exactly is the deadline?

By the third anniversary of its first registration. A car registered on 5 June 2023 must have its first MOT by 5 June 2026. Check the exact date free on GOV.UK if you are unsure.

Does the three years run from when I bought the car?

No. It runs from the date of first registration, which is on the V5C log book. A two-year-old used car has one year left, whoever owns it.

Do electric cars follow the same rule?

Yes. Electric cars are tested on the same timetable as petrol and diesel cars — first MOT by the third anniversary of registration, then every year.

Can I get the first MOT done early?

Yes, and it does not cost you time. Testing up to a month (minus a day) before the due date keeps the same renewal date for the next year.

What if I miss the first MOT date?

Do not drive the car, apart from going to a pre-booked test or to a garage for repairs. Book an MOT straight away, and remember you cannot renew the tax until the car has passed.

Not sure of your date? Run the free official check on GOV.UK with the number plate — it takes seconds, and it is far better than guessing from the log book.
