Van MOT: Class 4 vs Class 7, What It Costs and What Gets Checked

Last reviewed: November 2026 • Published by MOT History Check

A van MOT is not quite a car MOT. The test itself follows the same inspection manual, but vans are split into two classes by weight, the fee is different for each, and the things that fail a working van are not the things that fail a family hatchback. Most guides online either copy the car rules across or are written by a single garage trying to sell you a booking. This guide follows the official GOV.UK fees table and the DVSA inspection manual, so every figure below is the one the tester actually uses.

When does a van need its first MOT?

By the third anniversary of its registration, and then every year after that, the same rule as for a car. An MOT lasts for a year, and you can get it done up to a month (minus a day) before the current one runs out and keep the same renewal date for the following year. If you are not sure of the date, check it free on the official GOV.UK MOT history service with just the registration number, our guide to when your MOT is due explains the dates and the early-testing rule in full.

Driving a van without a valid MOT is not a minor matter: you can be fined up to £1,000, and you cannot tax the van or rely on its insurance without one. The only driving allowed without an MOT is to a pre-booked test, or to or from somewhere for repairs. Our guide to driving without an MOT covers the exceptions and the penalties.

Class 4 or class 7? How to tell from your V5C

The class of a van MOT is decided by one number: the design gross weight (DGW), the maximum the vehicle is designed to weigh when fully loaded, not what it happens to weigh on the day. GOV.UK splits goods vehicles like this:

Class 4, up to 3,000kg design gross weight. Small and medium vans: the Transit Custom, Vivaro, Berlingo and Transporter end of the market, plus most pickups, which count as dual-purpose vehicles in class 4. A motor caravan up to 3,000kg is class 4 too, our guide to motorhome and campervan MOTs covers those in detail.

Class 7, over 3,000kg up to 3,500kg design gross weight. The heavier end of the van market: long-wheelbase Transits, Sprinters, Crafters and Masters at the top of their weight range. A surprising number of large vans sit in class 7 rather than class 4, so do not assume yours is class 4 because it looks like an ordinary van.

To find out which yours is, check your V5C logbook for the revenue weight or gross vehicle weight, or look at the weights stamped on the manufacturer's VIN plate, usually under the bonnet or on a door pillar. Up to 3,000kg is class 4; 3,001kg to 3,500kg is class 7. If you get it wrong and book the wrong class, the centre cannot test the van, so check before you book, not on the day.

What a van MOT costs

The DVSA sets a maximum fee for each class, and no test centre can charge more than it. There is no VAT on the fee, and centres can charge less than the maximum, many do, especially for trade customers with several vans.

Class 4 van: £54.85 maximum. The same as a car.

Class 7 van: £58.60 maximum. Slightly higher, because the test needs heavier-duty equipment. Our MOT cost guide shows the full official fee table for every vehicle class.

If the van fails, the retest rules are the same as for a car: leave it at the test centre for repair and have it retested within 10 working days and there is no fee for the partial retest. Take it away and bring it back by the end of the next working day for one of the listed quick items, lights, mirrors, wipers, horn and the like, and there is no charge for that either. Our guide to what to do if your vehicle fails its MOT explains the retest rules and the appeal process.

What the tester checks on a van

Vans are tested against the same DVSA inspection manual for cars and light commercials as cars, so the checklist is familiar, brakes, steering, suspension, lights, tyres, seatbelts, windscreen, exhaust and emissions, body and structure, fuel system and registration plates. What changes is the emphasis, because a working van is heavier, carries loads, and wears its parts differently:

Brakes. Tested on a roller brake tester rated for the van's axle weights. Brake imbalance limits are the same as for cars, but the effort thresholds scale to the weight, and a van that has been carrying loads every day will show worn discs and pads sooner. The handbrake matters more on a van too, it has to hold a loaded vehicle on a slope.

Tyres and load rating. The minimum tread depth is the same 1.6 mm as a car, but the load index matters in a way it rarely does on a car: a class 7 van fitted with tyres rated below its axle load is a fail, even if the tyres look fine. Check the load index on the sidewall against the figure on the door pillar or chassis plate, and set the pressures for the load you carry.

Suspension. Heavy vans carry their stress on rear leaf springs or coil springs, and sag, broken leaves and worn bushes show up faster than on a car. A van that sits low at the back when loaded, or leans to one side, is telling you something before the tester does.

Headlamp aim. Checked with the van empty, but a van's headlights drift more under load than a car's. If they are aimed too high when empty, they will dazzle when the van is loaded, so get the aim checked if you have changed bulbs or had front-end work done.

Body and chassis corrosion. A heavier vehicle puts more stress on its structural members, so corrosion within 30 cm of a load-bearing point, suspension mountings, the chassis, seatbelt anchorages, is a bigger concern than the same rust on a car. On an older working van, corrosion around the rear spring hangers and the sills is what the tester looks for.

Emissions. A diesel van gets the smoke (opacity) test, not the petrol gas test, the same test as a diesel car, with the same DPF checks. Our guide to diesel car MOTs explains the smoke limits and the DPF rules in full; they apply to diesel vans the same way.

What is not in the test is the same as for cars: the condition of the engine, clutch and gearbox is not checked, and the MOT is not a service. A van can pass its MOT with an engine that is about to cost you thousands, the test only checks safety and emissions.

Can any garage do a class 7 MOT?

No, and this is the practical difference most van owners discover too late. A test centre must be authorised for class 7, and class 7 testing needs a bigger ramp and a brake tester rated for the heavier axle weights. Not every garage holds that authorisation, and a car-only centre cannot test a class 7 van at all. If your van is class 7, ask when you book, especially for a large van, a converted van, or one with a side-loading crane or tail lift.

Booking also matters for time. A class 7 test takes the same time as a class 4 test, but fewer centres offer it, so slots can be harder to find at short notice. Book a week or two ahead, and do not leave it to the last day of the old MOT.

Over 3,500kg? That is not an MOT any more

Goods vehicles over 3,500kg design gross weight sit outside the MOT system entirely. They go through the annual HGV test instead, sometimes called the annual test or plating and testing, at a DVSA goods vehicle testing station, with its own fee schedule and its own inspection. It is not an MOT, and an MOT centre cannot do it.

There is one exception worth knowing about: a zero-emission goods vehicle between 3,501kg and 4,250kg design gross weight is tested as class 7, with the same £58.60 maximum fee. That covers the heavier electric vans that would otherwise fall into HGV territory. If you run an electric van in that weight band, it still gets an MOT, just a class 7 one.

Five quick checks before a van MOT

Vans fail on small, cheap things far more often than on anything serious. A few minutes the day before the test covers most of them:

Lights. Switch everything on, headlamp main and dip, tail lights, brake lights, indicators both sides, number plate light. A blown bulb is the most avoidable failure there is, and on a van the rear lights are easy to forget.

Tyres. Check the tread is at least 1.6 mm deep, look for cuts and cracking, check the load index on the sidewall, and set the pressures. A van that has stood loaded all week can look fine and still be running on under-rated or under-inflated tyres.

Brakes and handbrake. Press the pedal, it should feel firm, not spongy, and pull the handbrake on a slope. If the van creeps, the handbrake needs adjusting before the test, not after it.

Wipers, washers and mirrors. Top up the washer fluid, check the wiper blades are not split, and make sure the mirrors are secure and give a view. A van's mirrors take more knocks than a car's, and a cracked or loose one is a fail.

Number plates and load area. Clean the plates so they are legible, and clear the load area so the tester can get to the rear lights, the spare wheel well and the chassis. A van full of tools and materials slows the test down and hides the things the tester needs to see.

Quick answers

How much does a van MOT cost?

The maximum fee is £54.85 for a class 4 van (up to 3,000kg design gross weight) and £58.60 for a class 7 van (over 3,000kg up to 3,500kg). Test centres can charge less, and there is no VAT on the fee.

How do I know if my van is class 4 or class 7?

Check the revenue weight or gross vehicle weight on your V5C logbook, or the weights on the manufacturer's VIN plate. Up to 3,000kg design gross weight is class 4; 3,001kg to 3,500kg is class 7.

When does a van need its first MOT?

By the third anniversary of its registration, and then every year after that, the same rule as for cars.

Can any garage do a class 7 van MOT?

No. The test centre must be authorised for class 7 and have heavier-duty equipment, a bigger ramp and a brake tester rated for the higher axle weights. Check when you book.

What if my van is over 3,500kg?

It does not get an MOT. Goods vehicles over 3,500kg design gross weight go through the annual HGV test instead. The exception is a zero-emission goods vehicle between 3,501kg and 4,250kg, which is tested as class 7.

To sum up: a van MOT costs at most £54.85 in class 4 or £58.60 in class 7, is due from three years old and every year after, and the class depends on the design gross weight on your V5C, up to 3,000kg is class 4, 3,001kg to 3,500kg is class 7. Check the weight before you book, make sure the centre is authorised for your class, and run through the lights, tyres, brakes and mirrors before you go.
