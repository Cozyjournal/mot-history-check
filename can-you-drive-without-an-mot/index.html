Can You Drive Without an MOT? Rules, Fines and the 2 Exceptions

Last reviewed: October 2026 • Published by MOT History Check

No, you cannot drive without a valid MOT. If your car's MOT has run out, you must not drive it on the road, and you cannot park it on the road either. The fine can be up to £1,000, and the police can stop you at any time. There are only two journeys you are still allowed to make, and this guide explains both of them in plain English.

It happens to a lot of people. Life gets busy, the date slips past, and one morning you realise the MOT ran out last week. So what do you do now — and can you still use the car while you sort it out? Here is the honest answer.

What the law says, in simple words

Once a car is more than 3 years old, it needs an MOT test every year. The MOT is a safety check. It shows the car met the legal safety and environmental standards on the day of the test.

If that yearly date has passed, the car has no valid MOT. From that moment:

  • You cannot drive the car on a public road.
  • You cannot park it on a public road either — it should be kept off the road, for example on a driveway or in a garage.
  • You cannot renew the vehicle's tax until it has passed a new MOT.
  • If you are caught driving it, you can be fined up to £1,000.

It does not matter if the car feels fine, or if it passed easily last year. If the date has passed, the rule is the same.

The 2 journeys you are still allowed to make

There are only two reasons you may drive a car with an expired MOT:

  1. Driving to a pre-booked MOT test. You can drive the car to an MOT test that you have already booked. The word “pre-booked” is the important part. Book the test first, then drive there. Driving around hoping to find a garage that can fit you in does not count.
  2. Driving to or from a place of repair. You can drive the car to a garage to be repaired, or bring it back from repairs, so it can be tested.

That is the full list. A trip to work, the school run, or a quick drive to the shops is not allowed, even if the test is booked for tomorrow.

What happens if you drive anyway?

Many people think the worst case is a small fine. It can be worse than that.

  • Fine: up to £1,000 for driving without a valid MOT.
  • Your car still has to be safe: an expired MOT does not let you off the hook for the car's condition. If the car has a dangerous fault, the penalties are much higher — a fine of up to £2,500, 3 penalty points, and you could even be banned from driving.
  • Tax problems: you cannot tax the car while the MOT has run out, so one missed date can quickly turn into two problems.

Put simply, one short trip is not worth the risk.

My MOT expired yesterday. What should I do now?

Do not panic, and do not keep driving. Just follow these steps:

  1. Keep the car off the road. Park it on a driveway or in a garage if you can.
  2. Book an MOT test at an approved test centre.
  3. Drive straight there on the day of your booking — that journey is allowed.
  4. Once the car passes, renew the tax if it is due, and you are fully legal again.

If the car fails the test, the garage will explain what needs fixing. Our guide on what to do if your car fails its MOT walks you through the next steps.

How to check your MOT date right now

Not sure when your MOT runs out? It takes less than a minute to check:

  • Use our free guide to check any UK vehicle's MOT history — you only need the number plate.
  • Or look at the date printed on your last MOT certificate.

One useful tip: you do not have to wait until the last day. You can get an MOT up to a month (minus a day) before the current one runs out and still keep the same renewal date for next year. Booking a little early is the easiest way to never end up in this situation. You can also read more about when your MOT is due.

Does every car need an MOT?

Not quite. A few vehicles do not need one yet, or do not need one at all:

  • Brand-new cars do not need an MOT until the third anniversary of their registration.
  • Some historic (classic) vehicles are exempt — there are rules about this, which we explain in our guide to MOT exemptions.
  • If a vehicle is declared off the road (SORN), it does not need an MOT while it stays off the road — but it must not be driven, apart from the two journeys above, if you later want to use it again.

Quick answers

Can I drive my car to the MOT station if the MOT has expired?

Yes, but only if the test is already booked. Book first, then drive straight there.

Can I drive to work if my MOT ran out yesterday and my test is next week?

No. There is no grace period. The only allowed journeys are to a pre-booked test or to and from a place of repair.

What is the fine for driving without an MOT?

You can be fined up to £1,000. If the car also has a dangerous fault, the fine can rise to £2,500, with 3 penalty points and a possible driving ban.

Can I park on the road if my MOT has expired?

No. A car with an expired MOT should not be driven or parked on the road. Keep it off the road until it passes.

How early can I get my next MOT?

Up to a month (minus a day) before the current one expires, and your renewal date stays the same.
