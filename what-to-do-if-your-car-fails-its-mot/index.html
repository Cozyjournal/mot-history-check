What to Do If Your Car Fails Its MOT

Last reviewed: October 2026 • Published by MOT History Check

Your car just failed its MOT. It feels like a big problem, but it usually is not. Thousands of cars fail every day, and most are fixed and back on the road within days. This guide shows you exactly what to do next, step by step, in plain English.

Step 1: Read your failure sheet carefully

When a car fails, the test centre gives you a paper called a refusal of an MOT test certificate. Your result is also saved in the national MOT database, so you can see it any time in your MOT history.

The sheet lists every problem the tester found. Each problem has a level:

  • Dangerous – the car is a direct risk to road safety or the environment. It must not be driven at all until it is fixed.
  • Major – the car may affect safety or other road users. It must be repaired as soon as possible.
  • Minor – no big effect on safety, but it should be repaired soon. Minor problems alone do not fail a car.
  • Advisory – not a fail item. It is a note about something that is wearing out and should be watched.

Your car fails if it has one or more dangerous or major problems. So look at your sheet and find those items first. They are the ones you must fix before the car can pass.

Step 2: Check if you are allowed to drive the car away

This is the part many people get wrong. After a fail, you can only take your car away if both of these are true:

  1. Your old MOT certificate is still in date, and
  2. No dangerous problems were listed on the failure sheet.

If both are true, you may drive the car away, for example to your home or to another garage for repairs. But be careful: even then, the car must still meet the basic legal standard of roadworthiness at all times. Driving a car in a dangerous condition is an offence on any day, MOT or not.

If your old MOT has already run out, or a dangerous problem was found, you cannot drive the car. It must stay where it is, be repaired there, or be moved by a recovery truck. Our guide on driving without an MOT explains the full rules and fines.

The fine for driving a car with a dangerous problem is serious: up to £2,500, 3 penalty points, and you can even be banned from driving. It is never worth the risk.

Step 3: Get the repairs done

You now have a simple choice: let the test centre repair the car, or take it to a garage you trust (if you are allowed to drive it away).

Before you agree to any work, ask for the price in writing. For a big repair, it is fine to get a second quote from another garage. Prices for the same job can be very different.

You only need to fix the dangerous and major items to pass. Minor and advisory items will not stop the car passing, but fixing them soon is still a good idea for safety. You can read more about the most common problems in our guide to MOT failure reasons.

Step 4: Book the retest the smart way

After the repairs, the car needs a retest. How much you pay depends on where the car was repaired and how fast you go back:

  • Car stays at the test centre for repair – if it is retested within 10 working days, you only need a partial retest and there is no fee.
  • You take the car away and bring it back by the end of the next working day – a partial retest on small items like lights, mirrors, the horn, seatbelts, tyres, wipers and number plates is free.
  • You take the car away and bring it back within 10 working days – you need a partial retest and the centre can charge a partial fee (usually around half the normal test fee).
  • You wait longer than 10 working days, or go to a different test centre – you need a full retest and you pay the full MOT fee again (up to £54.85 for a car).

So the smart move is simple: get the repair done quickly and take the car back to the same test centre within 10 working days. It saves you money.

One more rule: if your old MOT has run out, the only journeys allowed are driving to the repair garage or to a pre-arranged MOT test. Book first, then drive.

Step 5: Check your new result online

Once the car passes, the new result goes into the MOT database straight away. Check it in your MOT history and make a note of the new expiry date. To keep the same expiry date next year, remember you can book the next test up to a month (minus a day) early — our guide on when your MOT is due shows how that works.

What if you think the fail is wrong?

Sometimes a tester makes a mistake. If you believe your car should not have failed, you can appeal to the DVSA (the Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency).

Follow these steps:

  1. Talk to the test centre first and explain why you think the result is wrong.
  2. Do not get any repairs done yet. If the car is changed, the DVSA cannot check it properly.
  3. If you still disagree, fill in the DVSA complaint form within 14 working days of the test.
  4. The DVSA will contact you within 5 days. If they decide to recheck your car, you arrange a date and pay the full test fee again.
  5. If your appeal wins, your test fee is refunded.

Appeals are free to start, so if the fail looks clearly wrong, it is worth doing.

Quick answers

Can I drive my car after it fails its MOT?

Only if your old MOT is still in date and no dangerous problems were listed. Otherwise the car must be repaired before you drive it again, or moved by a recovery truck.

How much is a retest?

Often free. If the car stays at the test centre and is retested within 10 working days, the partial retest is free. A partial retest after taking the car away can cost a fee (usually about half). After 10 working days you pay the full fee again.

How long do I have to fix a failed car?

There is no fixed deadline, but the 10 working day window matters for a cheap or free retest. And if your MOT has run out, the car cannot be driven or parked on the road until it passes, except for driving to repairs or a pre-booked test.

What is the fine for driving a car that failed with a dangerous problem?

Up to £2,500, plus 3 penalty points, and you could be banned from driving.

Can I appeal an MOT fail?

Yes. Talk to the test centre first, do not repair the car, and send the complaint form to the DVSA within 14 working days of the test.
