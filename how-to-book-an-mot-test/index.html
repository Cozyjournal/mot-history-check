How to Book an MOT Test: Find a Centre, Pick a Date

Last reviewed: November 2026 • Published by MOT History Check

To book an MOT test takes just a few minutes: find an approved test centre, pick a date, and turn up with the car. There is no central GOV.UK booking service for cars — you book directly with a test centre, by phone or online. The two things worth getting right are the centre itself, which must be approved by the DVSA, and the date, because testing too early can move your renewal date. This guide walks through both, plus the cost, what to bring, and what happens on the day.

Before you book: check when your MOT is due

Before you ring anyone, find out your exact expiry date. Check the date printed on your current MOT certificate, or look it up in seconds on GOV.UK with just the registration number. Booking blind is how people end up testing weeks too early — or discovering, too late, that the certificate ran out last Tuesday. Our guide to when your MOT is due explains every way to check, including the free reminder service.

Step 1: Find an approved MOT test centre

An MOT can only be carried out by an approved test centre — one showing the blue sign with three white triangles. Any garage displaying that sign can test your vehicle, as long as it handles your vehicle class. There are a few ways to find one:

The official GOV.UK finder. GOV.UK has a service that lists approved test centres by postcode. It is the safest starting point, because it only shows authorised sites — search GOV.UK for “find an MOT test centre” and enter your postcode.

Your regular garage. If the garage that services your car is DVSA-approved, booking there is usually the simplest option — they already know the car, and they can fix anything it fails on.

National chains and dealerships. Chains such as Kwik Fit and Formula One Autocentres, and most main dealers, offer online booking and often have Saturday slots. They are useful if you want a fixed price up front or a slot outside working hours.

Map apps and search. Searching “MOT test near me” is quick for opening hours and reviews — but always check the garage actually displays the blue sign and is approved before you book.

Before you settle on a centre, check two things. First, that it can test your vehicle class: most centres do Class 4 cars, but a Class 7 van or a large motorhome needs a centre with the right facilities — our guide to van MOTs explains the Class 4 and Class 7 split. Second, ask the price up front and what happens if the car fails: some centres offer a free partial retest, others charge. Knowing that before you book is worth more than saving a few pounds on the test fee.

Step 2: Pick the right date

The date matters more than most people realise. You can get an MOT up to a month (minus a day) before it runs out and keep the same renewal date. GOV.UK gives this example: if your MOT runs out on 15 May, the earliest you can get an MOT and keep the same renewal date is 16 April.

Take the test earlier than that and it still counts — but your renewal date moves. If your MOT runs out on 15 May and you test on 14 April and pass, your new expiry date becomes 13 April the following year. You have effectively shortened your next certificate by a month, so there is no reason to test early unless you need to: for example, if you are going away, or the car needs work and you want time to fix it.

On the other hand, do not leave it to the last day. Test centres get busy, Saturday slots go first, and if the car fails you need time for repairs and a retest. Booking a week or two inside the one-month window is the sweet spot for most people: the renewal date stays the same, and there is still slack if something goes wrong. Centres take bookings weeks ahead, so if you want a Saturday morning, ring early — and if your current MOT has already expired, book a pre-arranged test, because driving to a pre-arranged test is one of the only two lawful journeys without a valid MOT. Our guide to an expired MOT covers that situation step by step.

Step 3: Book an MOT — phone, online or walk-in

How you book depends on the centre:

By phone. Still the most common way, especially at smaller garages. Have your registration number ready, and say what the vehicle is — car, van, motorcycle — so they can confirm they test that class. Ask the price, how long to allow, and whether there is somewhere to wait.

Online. Most chains and many independents now let you book on their website: enter the registration number and postcode, pick a date and time, and pay or reserve. Some offer a discount for booking online.

Walk-in. Some centres accept walk-ins, but a booked slot is always safer — turning up without one can mean a wasted journey, especially on a Saturday.

When you book, confirm three things: the total price, whether the fee includes a retest if the car fails, and how long the test will take. Our guide to how long an MOT takes explains why the answer is usually 45 to 60 minutes, and why the garage may still ask you to leave the car longer.

How much does an MOT cost?

The government sets a maximum fee, and no test centre can charge more than it. For a car the maximum is £54.85, and for a standard motorcycle it is £29.65 — and you do not pay VAT on the fee. Many centres charge less, often £25 to £45 for a car, and some offer the test at a discount if you book it with a service. A Class 7 van test costs up to £58.60. Our full guide to MOT costs lists the maximum fee for every vehicle class.

Be wary of a test priced far below the going rate with no mention of a retest. A cheap test that fails your car and then charges full price for the retest can cost more than a fairly priced one that includes a free partial retest. Ask before you book.

What to bring on the day

You do not need any paperwork to book or take the test — the centre works from your registration number. Taking your V5C logbook along can help if there is any question about the vehicle’s details, but the test itself is booked against the number plate. What does matter is the car itself:

Bring it reasonably clean. The tester needs to see the underside, the lights and the plates clearly. A car caked in mud can slow the test down, and a dirty number plate can itself be a fail.

Make sure there is fuel in it. The engine has to run for the emissions test, and some checks need the car driven onto the brake tester. Arriving on fumes is an avoidable problem.

Clear the boot and the cabin. The tester needs access to the spare wheel area, the seatbelts and the boot floor. A boot full of clutter gets in the way and wastes your slot.

Do the quick checks first. A dead bulb, low screenwash or a worn wiper blade are the most common fail items, and all of them take minutes to fix at home. Our pre-MOT checklist runs through fifteen checks you can do in your driveway the week before the test.

If you cannot take the car yourself

Some MOT centres will collect your vehicle for the test and return it afterwards — ask when you book, as it is not every centre that offers this. You can also get someone else to take the car in for you, as long as they are insured to drive it. Either way, the test is the same, and the result is recorded against the vehicle, not the driver.

What happens on the day

The test itself takes about 45 to 60 minutes. You can wait at the centre — most have a waiting area — or drop the car off and collect it later. You can watch the test from a viewing area, but you are not allowed to interrupt the tester.

If the car passes, the result goes straight into the MOT database, and you can ask the centre for a printed certificate. You may also get a list of minor or advisory items to keep an eye on. If it fails, you get a refusal certificate listing the faults, and you can find out whether a free or reduced-price retest applies: leave the car at the centre for repair and it is retested within 10 working days, and there is no fee for that partial retest. If you take the car away and bring it back by the end of the next working day for a retest on certain items — lights, wipers, tyres, mirrors and the like — there is no fee for that either. Our guide to what to do if your car fails its MOT covers the retest rules and the appeal process in full.

Booking in Northern Ireland

The process is different in Northern Ireland: MOTs there are booked through the DVA, not through a private test centre, and the test is carried out at a DVA test centre. If your car is registered in Northern Ireland, use the nidirect booking service rather than the Great Britain route described above.

Lorries, buses and trailers are booked differently

Heavy goods vehicles, buses and large trailers do not get a car-style MOT at all — they get an annual test, booked at an authorised testing facility (ATF), not at a car MOT centre. If that is what you need, the GOV.UK guide to the annual test for HGVs, buses and trailers explains how to book. And if you tow a caravan, note that the caravan itself needs no test at all — our guide to caravans and trailers and the MOT explains which trailers are tested and which are not.

Quick answers

How far in advance can I book an MOT?

You can book a slot as far ahead as the test centre will take bookings. The date that matters is the test date: you can take the test up to a month (minus a day) before your current MOT runs out and keep the same renewal date for next year.

Do I need to bring any documents to an MOT?

No documents are needed to book or take the test — the centre works from your registration number. Taking your V5C logbook along can help if there is any question about the vehicle’s details, but the test itself is booked against the number plate.

Can I book an MOT online?

Yes, many test centres and national chains let you book online by entering your registration number and postcode and picking a date and time. Smaller garages usually take bookings by phone, and some accept walk-ins — but a booked slot is always safer than turning up and hoping.

What if I cannot take the car to the test myself?

Some MOT centres will collect your vehicle for the test and return it afterwards — ask when you book. You can also get someone else to take the car in, as long as they are insured to drive it.

Can I book an MOT if my current one has already expired?

Yes. You can still book a test, and you are allowed to drive the car to a pre-arranged MOT test even with no valid MOT — that is one of the only two exceptions to the no-driving rule. You cannot drive it anywhere else until it has passed.

Booking an MOT is simple once you know the two rules that matter: use an approved centre, and test inside the one-month window so your renewal date stays put. Check the expiry date first, ask the price and the retest policy when you book, and run through the quick checks at home before you go — then the test itself is just 45 minutes of waiting.
