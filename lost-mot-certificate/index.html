Lost Your MOT Certificate? How to Get a Free Replacement

Last reviewed: November 2026 • Published by MOT History Check

A lost MOT certificate sounds like a problem, but it is one of the easiest car documents to replace. The paper copy is only a printout — the real record of your test sits in the official DVSA database, and it stays there whether or not you can find the sheet of paper. You can download a replacement free from GOV.UK in a few minutes, and this guide walks through exactly what you need, plus the other options if you cannot use the online service. If you are not sure which document you have lost — the VT20 pass, the VT30 failure notice or the VT32 advisory note — our guide to what each MOT certificate means explains them.

The short answer

If you have lost your MOT certificate, you do not need a new test and you do not need to pay anyone. Go to the official replacement service on GOV.UK, enter your number plate and the 11-digit reference number from your log book (V5C), and you can view, save or print a copy straight away. If you would rather deal with a person, any MOT centre can print a replacement for you, and the most it can charge is £10. Everything below explains each route in full.

How to get a replacement MOT certificate online

The free online service is run by GOV.UK and covers cars, motorcycles and vans tested in Great Britain. It takes a couple of minutes:

1. Find your V5C log book. You need the 11-digit document reference number printed in it. This is the same number used to unlock the full test-centre details in an MOT history check, and it is there to prove the request is coming from someone connected to the vehicle.

2. Open the official service. Go to GOV.UK: Replace a lost or damaged MOT certificate and start the service. Only use the GOV.UK page — plenty of unofficial sites offer to find your certificate for a fee, and there is never a fee for doing this yourself.

3. Enter the registration and the V5C number. Type in the number plate and the 11-digit reference number. The service finds the vehicle's current certificate from the test record.

4. Save or print your copy. You can download the certificate and print as many copies as you need. A home printout is a genuine copy of the official record — there is no watermark or code that makes the garage's original version more valid than yours.

Getting a copy from an MOT centre instead

If you do not have a printer, cannot find your V5C, or simply prefer to ask in person, any MOT centre can print a replacement certificate for you. It does not have to be the centre that carried out the original test — every approved centre can access the same record. Take the vehicle registration and the V5C reference number with you. The centre is allowed to charge for this, but the charge is capped at £10, and many will do it while you wait.

One thing worth knowing: a replacement is a copy of the existing record, not a new test. The centre will not look at your vehicle, the expiry date does not change, and nothing about your MOT status is affected. If your MOT has actually expired, a replacement certificate will not help — you need a new test, and our guide to when your MOT is due shows how to check the date first.

Do you actually need the paper certificate?

Most of the time, no. When your vehicle passes its test, the result is recorded in the MOT database straight away. The police, the tax system and insurers all work from that database record, not from the paper in your glovebox. You can tax your vehicle without ever producing the certificate, because your MOT status is checked against the record when you apply.

There are still a few situations where having a copy is handy. If you are selling the car, buyers often ask to see the certificate alongside the history, and handing over a printout makes the sale smoother — our guide to checking MOT history when buying a used car shows what buyers look for. Some employers and hire companies also ask for a copy if you drive for work. And if you simply like your paperwork in order, the free download takes two minutes. If you only want to confirm the expiry date or see the result, you do not need a certificate at all: checking the MOT history online shows the same record free, with just the number plate.

Lost your V5C log book as well?

This is the one situation that slows things down. Both the online service and MOT centres ask for the 11-digit V5C reference number, so without the log book you cannot use either route. The fix is to apply to the DVLA for a replacement log book first — GOV.UK has a service for getting a replacement V5C. Once the new log book arrives, take the reference number from it and get your certificate copy in the usual way. Until then, your MOT itself is still valid and still recorded; you are only missing the paperwork, not the test.

HGVs, trailers and public service vehicles

The online service does not cover heavy goods vehicles, trailers or public service vehicles. For HGVs and trailers, replacements are handled by post using the document replacement form VTG59, which costs £13. For public service vehicles, the equivalent form is VTP6 and the cost is £10. Both forms are linked from the same GOV.UK replacement page, so start there and follow the section for your vehicle type.

Quick answers

Is my MOT still valid if I've lost the certificate?

Yes. Your result is recorded in the official MOT database when the test is done, and that record is what counts. The paper certificate is just a copy of it, so losing the paper does not change your MOT status or its expiry date.

How much does a replacement MOT certificate cost?

Nothing if you use the free GOV.UK online service. If you ask an MOT centre to print one instead, it can charge up to £10. For heavy goods vehicles and trailers the postal replacement form costs £13, and for public service vehicles it costs £10.

What do I need to get a replacement MOT certificate online?

Two things: the vehicle registration (number plate) and the 11-digit reference number from the vehicle log book (V5C). Enter both on the GOV.UK replacement service and you can view, save or print the certificate straight away.

I've lost my V5C log book as well. Can I still get a copy?

Not until you have the V5C reference number, because both the online service and MOT centres ask for it. Apply to the DVLA for a replacement log book first, then use the number from the new V5C to get your certificate.

Do I need the paper certificate to tax or sell my car?

Not for tax — your MOT status is checked against the official record when you tax the vehicle, so the paper copy is never asked for. When selling, a printed certificate is not a legal requirement either, but buyers like to see one, and since the online copy is free it is worth printing before a viewing.

So if your MOT certificate has gone missing, don't pay anyone to find it and don't book a test you don't need. A lost MOT certificate is a two-minute job: number plate, V5C number, free download, done.
