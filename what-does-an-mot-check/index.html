What Does an MOT Check? The Full List of Test Items

Last reviewed: November 2026 • Published by MOT History Check

What does an MOT check? In short, it checks that your car meets the minimum road safety and environmental standards — nothing more, nothing less. The tester works through 21 areas set out in the DVSA inspection manual, from the brakes and tyres to the lights, the exhaust and the seatbelts, and records every problem as a minor, advisory, major or dangerous defect. This guide lists everything the test covers, in plain English, what it does not check, and how the results are graded — so you know exactly what your car will be judged on before you book.

Every car over three years old needs this test once a year, and the maximum fee a test centre can charge for a car is £54.85. You can watch the test from a viewing area, but you are not allowed to interrupt the tester. Our guide to how long an MOT takes explains what happens on the day, minute by minute.

What does an MOT check? The 21 test areas

GOV.UK publishes the official list of car parts checked at an MOT. Here it is, grouped so it is easy to follow, with what the tester actually looks for in each area.

Brakes

The brakes are tested on a roller brake tester, which measures how well they work and whether both sides brake evenly. The tester also checks the condition of the discs, pads, pipes and hoses where they can be seen, the parking brake, and the ABS and electronic stability control warning lights where the car has them. A brake warning light that shows a fault is a major defect — see our guide to which dashboard warning lights fail an MOT.

Steering and suspension

The steering is checked for wear, damage and excessive play in the wheel, column, rack and joints, and the power steering must work properly. The suspension — springs, shock absorbers and their mountings — is checked for damage, distortion and corrosion. The steering lock is tested too.

Tyres and wheels

Every tyre fitted to the car is checked for tread depth, condition and security. The tread must be at least 1.6mm deep across the central three-quarters of the tread, around the whole circumference, with no cuts, bulges or exposed cord. The tester also checks that the tyres are the right size and type for the car, and that the wheels are secure and undamaged. On cars first used on or after 1 January 2012, the tyre pressure monitoring system warning light is checked as well.

Lights and electrical equipment

Every exterior light is checked: headlights (including their aim, so they do not dazzle other drivers), sidelights, brake lights, indicators, fog lights, hazard lights and number plate lights. Headlamp cleaning and self-levelling systems are checked where fitted. Reversing lights are checked on cars first used from 1 September 2009, and daytime running lights on cars first used from 1 March 2018. The battery must be secure, and any electrical wiring the tester can see must be in good condition.

Windscreen, wipers, washers and mirrors

The windscreen is checked for damage in the driver’s view of the road — damage larger than 10mm in the driver’s direct view fails, as does damage larger than 40mm elsewhere on the screen. The wipers and washers must clear enough of the screen to give a clear view. The car must have at least the minimum number of mirrors, and they must be in good condition and securely fixed.

Seatbelts, seats and doors

Every seatbelt the car must have is checked: it must be fitted, suitable, in good condition, working properly and securely attached. The driver’s seat must adjust, every seat must be securely fitted, and seat backs must fix in the upright position. The doors must latch shut — the front doors must open from inside and outside, and the rear doors from outside. The bonnet must close securely, and the boot must close and latch.

Body, vehicle structure and general items

The body and structure are checked for excessive corrosion or damage in specific load-bearing areas, and for sharp edges likely to cause injury. The tester also checks that the speedometer works, the engine mountings are secure, and the horn works and is loud enough. A towbar, if fitted, is checked for security, condition and inappropriate repairs, including the 13-pin electrical socket.

Fuel system

The fuel system is checked for leaks, and the pipes and hoses must be secure and in good condition. The fuel cap must fasten and seal securely — a cap that will not seal is a fail.

Exhaust system and emissions

The exhaust must be secure and complete, without serious leaks, and not too noisy, with its catalyst in place if one was fitted as standard. The exhaust emissions are then tested against the limits for the car’s age and fuel type — petrol cars are tested for carbon monoxide and hydrocarbons, diesels for smoke. Electric cars have no exhaust emissions to test, and hybrids and electrics have their own checks; see our guides to the electric car MOT and the hybrid car MOT. Any fluid leak that puts the environment at risk is also recorded.

Registration plates and vehicle identification number (VIN)

Both number plates are checked for condition, fixing and colour, and the characters must be correctly formed and spaced. The vehicle identification number must be displayed on cars first used on or after 1 August 1980, and it must match the car’s records.

What an MOT does not check

An MOT is not a service, and it does not check the car’s general mechanical condition. In particular, the test does not cover:

The engine, clutch or gearbox. Their condition is not tested at all. A car can pass its MOT with an engine fault, as long as the fault does not affect the emissions test or a warning light the MOT checks.

The spare wheel and tyre. Spare wheels and tyres are not inspected, even if the spare is in the boot.

Engine oil and other fluid levels. The oil level, coolant level and screenwash level are not part of the test — although the washers themselves must work, so top the bottle up before you go.

Air conditioning, paintwork and cosmetics. The air conditioning is not tested, and scratches, dents and faded paint do not matter unless they create a sharp edge or hide corrosion in a structural area.

That is why a passed MOT is not a guarantee the car is in good condition — it only means the car met the minimum safety and emissions standards on the day of the test. A service is a separate job. If you are buying a used car, check its MOT history online as well: past failures and advisories tell you far more about a car than a single pass.

How MOT results are graded

Every problem the tester finds is recorded in one of four categories:

Dangerous. A direct and immediate risk to road safety, or a serious risk to the environment. The car fails, and it must not be driven until the problem is fixed.

Major. A problem that may affect the car’s safety, put other road users at risk, or put the environment at risk. The car fails.

Minor. A problem that has no significant effect on safety or the environment, but should be repaired as soon as possible. The car still passes.

Advisory. Something that could become a problem in future — a tyre wearing close to the limit, for example. The car passes, and the advisory is recorded on the certificate. Our guide to MOT advisories explained covers what to do with them.

If the car fails, you can appeal to the DVSA if you think the result is wrong, and you can get a free partial retest if the car is left at the test centre and retested within 10 working days. Our guide to common MOT failure reasons explains the retest rules in full.

What to check yourself before the test

Most MOT failures are for small things you can spot at home in ten minutes. Before you book, walk around the car and check the lights, the tyre tread and pressures, the wipers and washers, the horn, the mirrors and the number plates, and make sure the fuel cap fastens. Quick answers

Does an MOT check the engine?

No. The MOT does not check the condition of the engine, the clutch or the gearbox. Those are covered by servicing, not by the test. The MOT checks safety and emissions items only — brakes, tyres, lights, steering, suspension, seatbelts, the exhaust and the body.

Does an MOT check the tyres?

Yes. Every tyre fitted to the car is checked for tread depth, condition and security. The tread must be at least 1.6mm deep across the central three-quarters of the tread, around the whole circumference, with no cuts, bulges or exposed cord. The spare tyre is not inspected.

How long does an MOT take?

The test itself usually takes 45 to 60 minutes for a car. The total time at the garage can be longer if the car needs repairs or a retest, or if the garage is busy.

What happens if my car fails on one item?

If any item is recorded as a major or dangerous defect, the car fails. You can get a free partial retest if the car is left at the test centre and retested within 10 working days, or a free retest on certain items if you take it back before the end of the next working day.

Does an MOT check the spare tyre?

No. Spare wheels and tyres are not inspected during an MOT, even if the spare is in the boot. Only the tyres fitted to the car are checked.

So, what does an MOT check? Everything that keeps your car safe on the road and within the emissions limits — the brakes, steering, suspension, tyres, lights, windscreen, seatbelts, body, fuel system and exhaust — and nothing that does not. The engine, clutch, gearbox and spare tyre are not part of it. Run the home checks before you book, fix the small things first, and the test itself is usually the easy part.
