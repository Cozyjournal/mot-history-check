Top 10 MOT Failure Reasons

Written by MOT History Check editorial team Last updated: October 2024 Based on official DVSA Class 4 testing statistics

The top MOT failure reason in the UK is faulty lighting and signalling equipment, accounting for approximately 25% of all test fails. Nationally, almost 30% of vehicles fail their annual MOT on their first attempt, largely due to basic, preventable faults such as blown bulbs, worn wiper blades, tyre tread below 1.6mm, and low screenwash.

According to official figures compiled by the Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency (DVSA), millions of motorists fail their MOT each year for defects that could have been resolved at home in less than 10 minutes with zero specialist tools. Understanding these 10 categories helps you pass on the first attempt and avoid costly re-test delays.

1. Lamps, Reflectors & Electrical (25% of Fails)

Major / Dangerous

Lighting defects remain the single most common reason for MOT failure across all UK test centres.

  • What tester checks: Operation, brightness, color, aim, and condition of headlights (main and dipped beam), brake lights, indicators, fog lights, reversing lights, and rear registration plate illumination. Also verifies dashboard warning lights.
  • DIY check: Park facing a wall or garage door to check headlight reflections. Turn on hazard flashers and walk around the car. Have someone press the brake pedal or back towards a reflective shop window to check brake lights.
  • Fix idea & cost: Replacement halogen or filament bulbs cost £3 to £10 and take 5 minutes to fit. Headlight aim alignment at an autocentre typically costs £15 to £25.

2. Suspension & Shock Absorbers (13% of Fails)

Major

UK roads and potholes cause extensive wear to coil springs, ball joints, and anti-roll bar drop links.

  • What tester checks: Snapped coil springs, leaking dampers (fluid running down shock absorber body), cracked rubber suspension bushes, and excessive play in ball joints and wishbones.
  • DIY check: Press down firmly on each corner of the car. It should bounce once and immediately settle. If it continues oscillating, the shock absorber is weak. Listen for clunks or knocking noises over speed bumps.
  • Fix idea & cost: Anti-roll bar links cost £30 to £60 fitted. A broken coil spring costs approximately £90 to £160 per corner.

3. Brakes & Braking Performance (10% of Fails)

Major / Dangerous

Brake defects are classified as severe safety hazards under DVSA guidelines.

  • What tester checks: Minimum friction material thickness (brake pad friction layer must be at least 1.5mm), brake disc condition (grooves or cracking), hydraulic brake pipe corrosion or fluid leaks, handbrake efficiency, and brake imbalance across axles on a roller brake tester.
  • DIY check: Inspect brake fluid reservoir under the bonnet. Listen for grinding metal noises or high-pitched squeals when braking. Ensure the vehicle does not pull to one side under hard braking.
  • Fix idea & cost: Front brake pads cost £60 to £110 fitted. Front discs and pads combined typically range from £140 to £240.

4. Tyres & Tread Depth (8% of Fails)

Major / Dangerous

Tyres are the only contact point with the tarmac; testers apply rigorous safety thresholds.

  • What tester checks: Minimum statutory legal tread depth of 1.6mm across the central three-quarters of the tyre breadth around the entire circumference. Also checks for sidewall bulges, cuts longer than 25mm, exposed internal cord, tyre age, and correct size/speed ratings per axle.
  • DIY check: The 20p coin test: Insert a 20p coin into the main tread grooves. If the outer band of the coin is visible, your tyre is below the legal limit. Inspect inner and outer sidewalls for bulges.
  • Fix idea & cost: Budget tyres start from £50 to £70 fitted; premium brands range from £90 to £150 per tyre.

5. Visibility, Windscreen & Wipers (7% of Fails)

Minor / Major

Surprisingly, thousands of vehicles fail annually simply because the screenwash bottle was completely empty!

  • What tester checks: Glass damage in "Zone A" (the 290mm swept area directly in the driver's eyeline cannot contain damage exceeding 10mm). Rest of swept windscreen cannot have chips exceeding 40mm. Wiper blades must clear water cleanly without smearing or split rubber. Screenwash reservoir must spray washer jets onto the glass.
  • DIY check: Top up washer fluid with clean screenwash. Check wiper rubber for tears. Remove dangling air fresheners or sat-nav mounts from the driver's forward eyeline.
  • Fix idea & cost: Washer fluid costs £3 to £5. New wiper blade pairs cost £15 to £30 and clip on in 2 minutes. Small windscreen chips can often be repaired for free under comprehensive insurance policies.

6. Exhaust, Fuel & Emissions (5% of Fails)

Major

Emissions standards have grown strictly tighter, especially for diesel vehicles with particulate filters (DPFs).

  • What tester checks: Hydrocarbon (HC) and Carbon Monoxide (CO) levels for petrols; opacity/smoke levels for diesels. Testers physically verify that the factory Catalytic Converter or Diesel Particulate Filter (DPF) has not been removed or tampered with. Exhaust mounting brackets and exhaust leaks are also inspected.
  • DIY check: Ensure the engine is fully warmed up before arriving at the test station (take a 15-minute dual-carriageway drive). Check for blowing noises or rattles from underneath.
  • Fix idea & cost: Exhaust rubber mounts cost £5 to £15. Replacing an exhaust back box typically costs £70 to £140.

7. Steering Mechanism & Alignment (3% of Fails)

Major / Dangerous

Steering precision is critical for emergency collision avoidance.

  • What tester checks: Free play in the steering wheel, condition of tie rod ends and steering rack gaiters, power steering pump operation, fluid level, and electrical power steering dashboard warning lights.
  • DIY check: Check power steering fluid reservoir if applicable. Turn the steering wheel from lock to lock while stationary: verify it turns smoothly without groaning or binding.
  • Fix idea & cost: Outer track rod end replacement costs £50 to £90, including front-wheel tracking alignment.

8. Seatbelts & SRS Restraints (2% of Fails)

Major / Dangerous

Every fitted seatbelt must latch, lock under sudden deceleration, and retract correctly.

  • What tester checks: Webbing cuts, fraying, buckle latching mechanisms, automatic inertia reel locking, and airbag (SRS) malfunction indicator lamps.
  • DIY check: Pull each seatbelt out completely to inspect for fraying. Tug sharply to verify the inertia reel locks. Clip into buckle and ensure it releases cleanly. Clear clutter from rear seats so tester can access all belts.
  • Fix idea & cost: Replacement seatbelt stalk or buckle mechanism costs £40 to £85.

9. Body, Structure & Rust Corrosion (2% of Fails)

Major / Dangerous

Corrosion is classified under the DVSA "prescribed area" rule.

  • What tester checks: Structural rust or holes within 30cm of safety-critical mountings (such as suspension mounts, seatbelt anchors, subframe bolts, and door hinges). Also checks sharp jagged edges that could injure pedestrians.
  • DIY check: Look underneath sill panels and wheel arches for flaking rust or holes. Ensure all doors open and close securely from both inside and outside.
  • Fix idea & cost: Professional welding of sill or chassis sections ranges from £100 to £300 depending on complexity.

10. Registration Plates & VIN (1% of Fails)

Major

An avoidable administrative failure that catches out hundreds of motorists.

  • What tester checks: Number plates must be securely mounted, clean, unbroken, feature correct British Standard Charles Wright font with legal character spacing, and include the supplier postcode and BS AU 145e marking. VIN must be legible.
  • DIY check: Ensure plates are securely screwed or taped. Check for cracks or delamination where water has seeped between plastic layers. Clean away heavy road grime.
  • Fix idea & cost: A new road-legal pair of UK number plates costs £16 to £25 from registered plate suppliers (Halfords, local factors).
