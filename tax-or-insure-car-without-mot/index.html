Can You Tax or Insure a Car Without an MOT?

Last reviewed: October 2026 • Published by MOT History Check

Two different questions hide inside this one, and they have two different answers. Tax: no — if your car needs an MOT, you can't tax it without a valid one. Insurance: usually yes, you can buy a policy — but that policy won't make it legal to drive the car, and a claim could be at risk.

Here's how each part works.

Can you tax a car without an MOT?

No. If your vehicle needs an MOT, it must have a valid one before you can tax it. GOV.UK puts it plainly: you cannot renew your vehicle tax if your MOT has expired.

There's no clever way around this. Whether you tax online, by phone or at a Post Office, the system checks the MOT record for your registration automatically. No valid MOT on record, no tax. The certificate also needs to be valid when the new tax period starts — an MOT that runs out the day before your tax begins won't do.

The exceptions are the vehicles that don't need an MOT in the first place: cars under three years old, and vehicles that are officially exempt. Our guide to MOT exemptions explains which vehicles those are.

So if your MOT has run out and the tax is due, the order is fixed: get the car tested and passed first, then tax it.

Can you insure a car without an MOT?

In most cases, yes. Insurers will usually sell you a policy for a car that has no MOT. There are sensible reasons to have one: the car might be in the garage being prepared for its test, or kept off the road but still worth protecting against theft or fire.

But don't confuse "insured" with "legal". A policy doesn't give you permission to drive a car that has no MOT. The law on driving without a valid MOT still applies in full — you can be fined up to £1,000, and the only journeys allowed are to a pre-booked test or to and from repairs, as explained in our guide on can you drive without an MOT.

Will insurance pay out if there's no valid MOT?

This is the part that catches people out. Most insurance policies require the car to be kept in a roadworthy condition and used legally. If you have an accident while driving without a valid MOT, your insurer may:

  • refuse to pay for damage to your own car
  • reduce the amount they pay out
  • pay the other driver's claim, then try to recover the money from you

It isn't automatic in every case — it depends on your policy wording and what happened. But that's not a gamble worth taking. An MOT test costs a fraction of what even a small claim dispute could cost you. The safe working rule is simple: no valid MOT, no relying on your insurance.

The SORN alternative: declare the car off the road

If the car won't be used for a while, there's a cleaner option than letting everything lapse. You can make a SORN — a Statutory Off Road Notification — which tells DVLA the vehicle is off the road.

While a SORN is in place:

  • you don't need to tax the vehicle
  • you don't need an MOT for it
  • it must be kept on private land, such as a driveway or a garage — not on a public road

You can make a SORN free on GOV.UK with the reference number from your vehicle log book (V5C). And when you're ready to use the car again, the SORN doesn't trap you: you can still drive the car to a pre-booked MOT test, which is the first step back to being fully legal.

One warning: if your tax has run out and you do nothing — no tax, no SORN — DVLA can fine you. Declaring SORN takes minutes and avoids that.

Getting back on the road: the right order

If your MOT has expired and the car is untaxed or SORN, here's the sequence that keeps you legal at every step:

  1. Insure the car. You must be insured to drive it, even for the short trip to the test centre.
  2. Book an MOT. Make sure it's a real booking — the exception only covers pre-arranged tests.
  3. Drive straight to the test. No stops, no detours, no other journeys.
  4. Once it passes, tax it. Taxing the vehicle cancels the SORN automatically, and you're fully legal again.

Quick answers

Can I tax my car if the MOT expires next week?

Yes, as long as the MOT is still valid on the date your new tax starts. If it expires before the tax start date, you'll need to get the car tested first.

My car is two years old. Do I need an MOT to tax it?

No. Most cars don't need an MOT until the third anniversary of their registration, so a two-year-old car can be taxed without one. See MOT exemptions for the full list of vehicles that don't need a test.

Does my insurance stop the moment the MOT runs out?

Not automatically. Your policy doesn't simply vanish. The risk is at claim time: your insurer may refuse or reduce a payout if the car had no valid MOT and was being driven illegally. Check your policy wording if you're unsure.

Do I need insurance for a car that's on a SORN?

If the car is kept entirely off public roads, insurance isn't legally required — though some owners keep cover for fire and theft. The moment you drive it, even to a pre-booked MOT, it must be insured.

Can I drive an untaxed car to a pre-booked MOT?

Yes — the exception for driving to a pre-booked test still applies to a SORN or untaxed vehicle, as long as the car is insured and you drive straight there. Once it passes, tax it straight away.

The bottom line

No MOT means no tax — the system simply won't let you. Insurance is different: you can buy it, but it won't protect you properly if you drive without a valid MOT. If the car is off the road for a while, declare a SORN instead. And before you plan any of it, check your car's status free on the official GOV.UK service so you're working from the real dates.
