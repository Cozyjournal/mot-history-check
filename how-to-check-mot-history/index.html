How to Check MOT History (Free, 2 Minutes)

Written by MOT History Check editorial team Last updated: October 2024 Verified against DVSA Testing Data

Ready to check plate : Follow Step 1 below to open the official GOV.UK checker and paste or type this registration number into the DVSA search field.

To check any UK vehicle's MOT history for free, visit the official GOV.UK MOT history check service and enter the vehicle's registration number (number plate). In under 2 minutes, you will see the vehicle's complete digital record: every past pass and fail date, verified annual mileage, safety advisories, and the exact MOT expiry date since 2005.

What You Need Before You Start

Checking vehicle roadworthiness on the Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency (DVSA) public database is remarkably straightforward:

  • Vehicle Registration Mark (VRM): The number plate of the car, van, or motorbike (e.g. AB12 CDE).
  • No Logbook (V5C) Needed: You do not need the V5C registration certificate, chassis number (VIN), or V11 tax reminder to view past test histories.
  • No Login or Fee: The service is completely free and requires no registration or debit card.

Note: A V5C 11-digit document reference number is only requested if you wish to download and print a replacement paper MOT certificate PDF. For viewing inspection histories and mileage charts, the registration number alone is sufficient.

Step 1: Go to the Official GOV.UK MOT Check Portal

Always use the genuine UK Government portal. Third-party sites often copy this data and charge unnecessary fees of £5 to £15.

Access the official service directly here:

Open GOV.UK Check MOT History Service ↗

Official URL: https://www.gov.uk/check-mot-history (Secure HM Government portal).

Step 2: Enter the Registration Number

Once on the GOV.UK landing screen, click the green "Start now" button. On the next screen:

  1. Type the vehicle's number plate into the input field. Spaces do not matter (e.g. EA18XYZ or EA18 XYZ both resolve accurately).
  2. Click the "Continue" button.
  3. The portal will confirm the vehicle make, model, and colour (e.g. Volkswagen Golf, Blue) and ask you to confirm if this matches the car you are searching.
  4. Click "Continue" to display the full test certificate log.

Step 3: Read and Understand the History

The result page is split into two critical zones: current status at the top, followed by chronological annual test cards below.

1. Top Banner: MOT Status & Expiry

Displays whether the vehicle is currently road legal.

  • Valid (Green): Shows the exact date the MOT expires. You can legally drive the vehicle until 23:59 on that day. Check our When Is My MOT Due guide to see early renewal windows.
  • Not Valid / Expired (Red): The vehicle has no current MOT. Driving it on public roads is unlawful except when travelling directly to a pre-booked MOT test.

2. Individual Test Cards: What Each Line Means

For each annual test, you will see:

  • Date Tested: The exact date the inspector conducted the test at an authorised DVSA testing facility.
  • Result (PASS or FAIL): Clear status badge indicating whether the vehicle met minimum statutory road safety standards.
  • MOT Test Number: A unique 12-digit reference identifying that specific test record in the national database.
  • Mileage: The exact odometer reading recorded by the tester. Tip: Verify that the mileage climbs logically every year.
  • Defects and Advisories: Categorized items flagged during inspection. See our detailed breakdown in the MOT Advisories Explained guide.

What If There Is No MOT History?

If the GOV.UK search returns "No MOT history found", this usually happens for one of four legitimate reasons:

  1. The vehicle is under 3 years old: Brand-new cars registered in Great Britain do not require an MOT test until their 3rd anniversary of registration. Check the vehicle's first registration date on the V5C logbook.
  2. The car is registered in Northern Ireland: Northern Ireland uses the Driver and Vehicle Agency (DVA), which does not record historical data on the GB DVSA tool. Tests can be verified via the NI Direct portal.
  3. Imported or newly registered vehicle: If a vehicle was recently imported from abroad or allocated a brand new registration mark, it can take up to 5 working days for the DVLA database to sync with DVSA.
  4. Historic Vehicle Exemption: Vehicles built or first registered more than 40 years ago that have not been substantially altered are legally exempt from annual MOT testing.

Can You Check MOT History Without a Registration Number?

No, you cannot. The official DVSA MOT history system indexes records solely through the vehicle registration mark. You cannot search by Vehicle Identification Number (VIN / Chassis Number), engine code, or owner name.

If you are considering a used vehicle listed on an online marketplace (such as AutoTrader, eBay Motors, or Facebook Marketplace) and the seller has blurred or hidden the number plate, politely ask them for the registration before travelling. Reputable sellers will readily provide the registration mark. If a seller refuses, treat it as a significant red flag.

Used Car Buyer Pro-Tip: The Mileage Curve Audit

When reviewing past tests, jot down the mileage from each year. If a vehicle registered 15,000 miles per year between 2018 and 2021, and suddenly registers only 1,000 miles in 2022 followed by an odometer discrepancy, inspect the dashboard closely. Mileage rollbacks are illegal, but odometer tampering remains one of the most common used-car frauds in the UK.
