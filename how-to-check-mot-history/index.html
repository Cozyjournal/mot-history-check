How to check MOT history online

Last reviewed: October 2026 • Published by MOT History Check

Quick number plate check

Enter any UK registration below to lookup past test results and advisory items.

Checking an MOT history is completely free and requires only the vehicle registration number. You can view every pass, fail, recorded mileage reading, and advisory note logged on the national database since 2005.

Whether you are considering buying a second-hand car, checking when your personal vehicle is next due for an inspection, or verifying test records before visiting a garage, the official online record gives you an immediate picture of vehicle roadworthiness. Below is the complete step-by-step walkthrough of how the service works, what information is shown, and when you need additional documentation.

Step 1: Obtain the vehicle registration number

Every UK vehicle test record is linked directly to its registration plate. You do not need the current registered keeper's permission, nor do you need to own the vehicle to review its public test history. To get started, make sure you have the exact number plate.

When searching for a vehicle online on a classified listings portal (such as Autotrader, eBay Motors, or Gumtree), sellers frequently include the number plate in either the description text or photos. If a seller deliberately blanks out or hides the registration plate in photos and refuses to provide it upon request, treat this with caution before arranging a physical viewing.

Step 2: Enter the plate into the checker or GOV.UK

Once you have the registration plate, you can submit it through our lookup tool above or navigate directly to the official government portal at gov.uk/check-mot-history.

The system validates standard UK formats (ranging between two and eight characters). Spaces and capitalisation are handled automatically. Because the central MOT database is managed by the Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency (DVSA), the information returned is pulled straight from the electronic records created by nominated testers at authorised garages across Great Britain.

Step 3: Understand the results and test details

Once the record loads, you will receive an itemised chronological summary of every official inspection conducted on the vehicle. For cars, motorcycles, and light commercial vans, records date back to 2005. For heavy goods vehicles (HGVs), trailers, buses, and public service coaches, records date back to 2018.

For each test record, the system displays the following key data points:

  • Date of the test: The exact date the vehicle underwent its annual MOT examination.
  • Test outcome: A clear status showing whether the vehicle PASSED or FAILED.
  • Expiry date: If the vehicle passed, the date the certificate remains valid until.
  • Recorded odometer mileage: The mileage entered by the testing technician at the moment of inspection.
  • Defects and advisory notes: Itemised lists of any minor defects, major failures, dangerous faults, or advisory observations noted by the tester.

Checking the expiry date is essential because driving after it has passed is illegal; read our guide on whether you can drive without an MOT to see the penalties and only allowed journeys.

When do you need the 11-digit V5C reference number?

A common point of confusion is whether the V5C vehicle logbook is required. You do not need the logbook simply to review whether a vehicle passed or failed, what advisories were given, or what the mileage was. That information is completely open to the public using the registration number alone.

However, if you want to know which specific garage or test centre carried out each test, or if you need to download a replacement paper certificate or see specific minor defect breakdown details, GOV.UK requires the 11-digit document reference number printed inside the vehicle's latest V5C logbook. This safeguard prevents random individuals from discovering where an unfamiliar car was tested while allowing legitimate owners and registered keepers full access.

Checking for outstanding safety recalls

In addition to regular annual inspection records, the DVSA MOT system integrates with manufacturer safety recall databases for participating vehicle brands. If a car is subject to an active safety recall that has not yet been rectified by an authorised franchise dealership, a prominent alert will often appear at the top of the test overview page.

If you see an active recall banner, contact an authorised franchise dealership for that vehicle make. Safety recalls are legally required to be repaired free of charge by the manufacturer, regardless of how old the car is or how many owners it has had.

Coverage in Northern Ireland

It is important to note that vehicles registered and tested in Northern Ireland follow a slightly different administrative system operated by the Driver & Vehicle Agency (DVA) rather than the DVSA. Northern Ireland test records on the national portal start in 2017. Furthermore, cars in Northern Ireland do not require their first MOT test until they reach four years of age (compared to three years in England, Scotland, and Wales).

Key checks to make before buying a used vehicle

When reviewing past MOT records on a prospective purchase, follow these essential guidelines:

  1. Compare odometer trends: The mileage must increase logically with each passing year. If a 2022 test shows 60,000 miles and the 2023 test shows 42,000 miles, the odometer may have been clocked or incorrectly recorded.
  2. Review recurring advisories: Look for issues that appear year after year (such as slight play in steering ball joints or minor oil leaks). If the same advisory appears three years running, previous owners may have deferred essential preventative maintenance.
  3. Check how quickly failures were addressed: If a car failed on dangerous brake defects and passed a retest on the exact same day, ask whether genuine replacement parts were fitted or if superficial fixes were applied.
